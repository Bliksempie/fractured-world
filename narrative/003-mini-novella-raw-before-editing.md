## Ghosts by the Stream

{{player}} woke instantly, hand over {{child}}'s mouth. Two figures—a man and a girl—emerged from the mist. Ragged. Starved. Armed. Tension stretched taut as wire. Then {{player}} glimpsed the patch on the man's jacket: a red dog on white.

Home.

## The Roots Awaken

The storm rolled in like a warning.

By dusk, the heavy grey clouds had dropped low enough to scrape the crumbling rooftops, the scent of rain stitching itself into the endless stench of decay.

The road twisted into the skeletal remains of a township—not a town anymore, not really. Just bones picked clean by time.

{{player}} tightened {{player-pronoun-possessive}} grip on {{child}}'s shoulder as they moved through the sagging ruins.

A town hall loomed ahead, half-swallowed by moss and fungal rot, its once-proud stone facade cracked and pitted. The crest above its doors—a lion rampant, maybe, or just a smear of old paint—was barely visible beneath the creep of Gravebloom.

"Shelter," {{player}} murmured, more to {{player-reflexive}} than to {{child}}. They slipped inside. The heavy doors groaned like old bones.

Inside, the air was thicker. Clammy. Every surface was coated in dust, debris, and stranger things: symbols carved into the walls with bone or rusted knives. Crude shrines cobbled from chairs and desks, arranged in circles around blackened candles.

{{child}} clung closer. {{child-pronoun-possessive-cap}} breathing quickened.

A sudden click-click echoed down the hall.

{{player}} froze and signaled {{child}} down behind a toppled filing cabinet. They waited. From the far end of the ruined hall, they saw the movements. Figures, hunched and twitching, shuffled forward—not quite human, not quite monster. Their skins were patched with fungal bloom, but their eyes—their eyes burned with fractured intelligence.

**Gravebloomers**.

The mindless ones. The Mimics. {{player}} had only heard about them through **The Living Chronicles**.

They staggered, tilting their heads at unnatural angles, moving in eerie pantomimes of long-forgotten human gestures—waving, clapping, giggling dryly without joy. Behind them, another figure emerged—taller, heavier, bearing itself with terrible purpose. A Bishop.

{{player}} had heard the stories. Some called them priests of the bloom. Others said they were just those infected who had lasted long enough to forget what they once were. The Bishop barked a command—guttural and wet—and the Mimics scattered, searching. Hunting.

{{player}} didn't wait. {{player-pronoun-possessive-cap}} grabbed {{child}}'s wrist, staying low, weaving through the rubble toward a side door sagging open on rusted hinges. Beyond the doorway they saw a vault—a relic from when governments still believed they could outlast the world's collapse. Inside, it reeked of mold and rust, but something gleamed faintly atop a pedestal of stacked stone: A fragment.

Old leather, weathered and cracked, bearing strange glyphs burned deep into its surface. And beside it—a battered keycard, its corner chipped, an emblem of the Old World government stamped in fading ink. Footsteps clicked closer.

Without hesitation, {{player}} swept the fragment and keycard into {{player-objective}} pack and motioned for {{child}}. They ran. The Bishop howled behind them—a sound part human, part monster—and the ruins of the town seemed to erupt with life. Shapes moved in the dark. Hands clawed from broken windows.

They ran through the rain until they could no longer hear the Mimics' laughter trailing them, until their lungs burned raw. Until there was only the sound of the storm, and the beat of their own hearts. When their heartbeats have calmed down, {{player}} looked at the fragment.

## The Watchers of the Hills

The stream behind them thinned into a muddy trickle, swallowed by rising mist. The road twisted upwards now, into broken foothills where the bones of the old world—rusted radio towers, shattered pylons—jutted against the bruised sky like broken teeth.

That night, they took shelter in the ruins of an old roadside diner. The windows were gone, the booths rotted to their springs. The old neon sign still clung stubbornly to the roof, half-collapsed, spelling something obscene in the flickering dark. It was there, peering through a broken window, that they saw them:

Lights.

Not the steady searchlights of soldiers, nor the frantic flares of scavengers. These lights moved slow. Strange. Lanterns bobbing from unseen hands atop the distant radio towers. Sometimes the lights flickered and vanished. Sometimes they danced, erratic and frantic, before dying into darkness altogether.

{{child}} pressed {{child-objective}} nose against the glass, breath misting the pane.

"{{player-title}}... are they survivors?"

{{player}} didn't answer immediately. {{player-pronoun-possessive-cap}} instincts prickled cold along {{player-objective}} spine.

Survivors didn't move like that. Survivors didn't hang themselves high like offerings to dead gods.

He set a steady hand on {{child}}'s shoulder. Leaned closer. Across the dead fields, just for a moment, he saw them: figures clustered at the base of the towers. Shrouded in cloaks and fungal bloom. Standing utterly still beneath the swaying lights. Watching. Waiting.

Maybe the rumors were true. Maybe the Gravebloomers had sentries now.

{{player}} made {{player-objective}} choice.

He tightened {{child}}'s scarf around {{child-objective}} neck, checked the battered knife at {{player-objective}} belt, and whispered, "Stay close. Eyes sharp. No talking unless we have to."

They left the diner behind and hiked up the muddy slope, boots sinking into the soaked earth, fingers numb with cold.

As they neared the towers, the lights grew clearer. Not electric. Chemical. Old-world lanterns, their flames guttering weakly against the mist. Each lantern was tied to a crude effigy at the base of the towers—twisted frames of wire and bone, draped in fungal growths, humming with a sound that vibrated low in the chest.

{{child}} shivered.

"It's like they're... feeding the towers," {{child-pronoun}} whispered.

{{player}} said nothing. Words would have changed nothing. Ahead, the watchers stood.

Their bodies were patchworks of human and horror—scars etched in intricate fungal patterns, faces hidden behind shredded masks and cloaks. They stood perfectly still, heads upturned toward the towers, humming in one terrible, united voice.

Not frenzied. Not feral. Organized. And at the center of it all—bound to the largest tower—a massive fungal mass pulsed faintly, like a heart still beating long after death.

{{player}} gritted {{player-objective}} teeth.

This was no mindless infection. This was ritual. Territory marking. Communication. The Gravebloomers had evolved.

Just as {{player}} turned to signal retreat, {{child}} tugged {{player-objective}}'s sleeve and pointed.

Half-buried at the base of a fallen tower, something metallic gleamed faintly beneath the grime—a supply crate, stamped with an emblem {{player-pronoun}} hadn't seen since the old world had gasped its last breath: A broken sun. Old World tech. Risky. Maybe vital.

{{player}} weighed the choice.

The world watched from the towers, humming its terrible song.
The gravebloomed sentries stood silent, blind—for now.

The choice lay heavy in {{player-objective}}'s gut.

## Fragment 782b-d4

{{player}} dragged it out from under the collapsed tower frame, boots scraping against the fractured concrete. Something about it felt wrong—not cursed, just... stripped. As if someone else had already been there and left the shell behind.

With a breath, {{player-pronoun}} opened it.

A chill. The kind that didn't come from wind.

"It means," {{player}} said, "we're not alone out here."

They moved quickly, abandoning the ruins without rest. The wrapped item in the crate was a data shard — Old World tech, encrypted. Fragile. Dangerous. It had been meant for someone else. But it was theirs now.

As they pushed into a forested stretch of the dead zone, {{child}} stopped suddenly.

"Did you hear that?"

{{player}} froze. Just behind them, the sound of footsteps in gravel... perfectly timed to their own. When they stopped — so did it.

No sign of a Mimic. No echo in the air.

That night, they made camp in a collapsed underpass, fireless and silent. As {{player}} inspected the shard beneath flickering flashlight, a single line blinked onto its surface.

## The Memory That Wasn't

They walked until the trees gave way to ash.

Not fire-scorched — not recently. This place had died years ago. A hollow basin carved by time and abandonment, where grass refused to grow and the ground crunched like old paper. Nothing lived here. Not even whispers.

{{player}} stopped walking.

"Come on, {{player-title}}! You're so slow!"
