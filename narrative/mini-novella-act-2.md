# Fractured World - a mini novella - Act 2

## Fragment 782b-d4

The crate was lighter than it should've been.

{{player}} dragged it out from under the collapsed tower frame, boots scraping against the fractured concrete. Something about it felt wrong—not cursed, just... stripped. As if someone else had already been there and left the shell behind.

{{child}} lingered near the edge of the crumbling stairwell, half-hidden behind a buckled steel beam, chewing {{child-pronoun-possessive}} lip.

"You think it's safe to open?"

{{player}} didn't answer right away. The latch was cracked but not broken. A crude scratch marked its top — not a symbol, just a line. Deliberate. As if someone had drawn a blade across it to warn, not to seal.

With a breath, {{player-pronoun}} opened it.

Rusted cans. An empty injection pack. A folded note, stained in the corner. And at the bottom, a wrapped object sealed in plastic and tape. Heavy. The kind of weight that didn't come from food or water.

{{child}} crept closer as {{player}} unfolded the note. The handwriting was jagged, erratic, but clear enough to read.

"You're late. But it doesn't matter. It's watching now. If you hear your own voice — run."

A chill. The kind that didn't come from wind.

"What does it mean?" {{child}} asked, eyes wide.

"It means," {{player}} said, "we're not alone out here."

They moved quickly, abandoning the ruins without rest. The wrapped item in the crate was a data shard — Old World tech, encrypted. Fragile. Dangerous. It had been meant for someone else. But it was theirs now.

As they pushed into a forested stretch of the dead zone, {{child}} stopped suddenly.

"Did you hear that?"

{{player}} froze. Just behind them, the sound of footsteps in gravel... perfectly timed to their own. When they stopped — so did it.

No sign of a Mimic. No echo in the air.

But something knew.

That night, they made camp in a collapsed underpass, fireless and silent. As {{player}} inspected the shard beneath flickering flashlight, a single line blinked onto its surface.

Fragment 782b-d4 confirmed. Classification: Influence Memory.

And then, a voice. Not from the shard. From beyond the concrete veil.

"I carried that once. Before it burned through the last of me."

It wasn't a threat. Not yet.

It was a complication.

Morning bled into the underpass slowly — no sun, just the shifting gray behind soot-stained clouds.

{{player}} hadn't slept. The shard sat in {{player-pronoun-possessive}} lap, inert again, as if the voice from the night before had never spoken. But {{player-pronoun}} remembered. The gravel-soft steps. The voice that didn't echo.

{{child}} stirred under the thermal wrap, blinking sleep from {{child-pronoun-possessive}} eyes. Not fear this time — suspicion. The kind only a child who has seen too much can carry.

"You didn't sleep," {{child}} said.

"Didn't want to miss anything."

"That's what people say when they do miss something."

The shard blinked once. A pulse. Then silence.

They packed quickly. The forest ahead had not changed — the trees still leaned as if pulled toward some gravity not born of earth. The path forked, one side overgrown and brittle, the other paved but marked by a warning: a rusted sign with three red dots and no text. Someone had scratched through it.

{{player}} hesitated. The paved road was clearer — easier with {{child}} in tow. But the dots...

Before {{player}} could choose, the shard blinked again. A new line scrolled across its damaged surface.

Route recognized. Survivor Protocol 4: Negotiation Required.

"Negotiation with what?" {{player}} muttered.

"With who," a voice replied from the trees.

They turned fast — weapon raised. A figure stepped out from the mist. Unarmed. Tall, gaunt, wrapped in multiple layers of scavenged fabric and filtration cloth.

"You have something that belongs to the old world," the stranger said. "And something the new world wants back."

"You following us?" {{player}} asked.

The figure tilted its head.

"No. You walked where you were seen. The shard marks you now."

"You want it?"

"No," the figure said. "I want to listen to it."

{{child}} stepped behind {{player}}, fingers gripping tightly.

"Let {{child}} go," the stranger said. "Just for a moment. So we can listen without fear clouding it."

The shard pulsed again — this time audibly. A soft hum, almost like breathing.

"That's not how this works," {{player}} said. "We listen together. Or not at all."

The figure paused. Then lowered {{partner-pronoun}} hood. Older than expected. Pale eyes. Lips cracked with silence.

"Then be warned," the figure said. "The next time it speaks, it won't be to you. It'll be through you."

And then the stranger turned and vanished — not into the trees, but into the fog that hadn't been there a moment before.

"What did they mean?" {{child}} asked.

"It means we walk," {{player}} said, "and we don't stop until we find a place where the fog doesn't follow."

"But what if it's not the fog?"

"Then we keep walking anyway."

## The Memory That Wasn't

Title: The Memory That Wasn't
File Reference: Fragment 782b-d6 (Revised)

They walked until the trees gave way to ash.

Not fire-scorched — not recently. This place had died years ago. A hollow basin carved by time and abandonment, where grass refused to grow and the ground crunched like old paper. Nothing lived here. Not even whispers.

{{player}} stopped walking.

The shard — silent since the encounter with the stranger — began to warm in {{player-pronoun-possessive}} pocket. Not from heat, but something else. Like static. Like breath. A pulse that wasn't rhythmic, but remembered.

{{player}} pulled it out. No light. No sound. Just a weight that tugged at the base of the skull — and then, without warning:

The sound of waves.

Clean light.

A table. A warm hand. Laughter. A glass bottle sweating on ceramic tile.

A moment. Not a memory {{player}} recognized — but one {{player}} felt as real. The child — smaller, younger — ran across wet stone under a golden sky.

"Come on, {{player-title}}! You're so slow!"

It wasn't {{child}}'s voice. Not quite. Too high. Too free.

And {{player}} moved to follow.

But something grabbed their ankle.

Sudden cold. Drowning air. Hands pulling at {{player-pronoun-possessive}} throat — hands made of barbed wire and fog. The scene shattered like ice.

{{player}} collapsed forward in the basin. Dirt in {{player-pronoun-possessive}} mouth. Fingernails clawed earth like they were still underwater.

"Are you okay?" {{child}} knelt beside {{player-objective}}, wide-eyed.

"I... saw something," {{player}} said. "But it wasn't mine."

The shard in {{player-pronoun-possessive}} hand had gone cold. Slick with condensation, though the air was dry.

There was something etched faintly along its edge now — not letters. Not language. Just a series of jagged impressions that felt like warning. Like the edge of a scream that never made it out.

"It did something to you," {{child}} said softly.

"No," {{player}} said. "It let me feel something. That's worse."

They sat in silence. The wind didn't stir, but still there was a sound — distant, out of place. High, metallic.

Wind chimes.

No breeze. No movement. Just the hollow, swaying tones of a memory trapped in metal, singing a song no one left alive remembered.
