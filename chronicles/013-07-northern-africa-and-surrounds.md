# Northern Africa and Surrounds

Home to deserts older than time and cities built upon the bones of ancient empires. **Northern Africa**, with its vast open spaces, initially resisted the spread better than most. Nomadic tribes, desert fortresses, and hidden oases delayed the inevitable. But trade routes and urban centers crumbled one after another, and the Sahara itself began to swallow the last remnants of humanity like a patient, hungry god.

The Sahara has no borders. But under its skin, oases bloom where blood was spilled.

## Natural Disasters

Sandstorms, droughts and seismic activity.

## Minerals and Resources

Phosphates, oil, salt and spice routes.

## Survival Insight

Ancient Berber fortresses or desert tombs have become safeholds or cult sanctuaries.

## Nomadic instinct. Wind-scarred memory.

Survivors of the shifting sands know how to vanish when needed. Starting here grants a minor bonus to travel efficiency in open terrain, and resistance to heat and dehydration effects. Certain sacred ruins may reveal more to players from this region.

## Starting Inventory

📜 Faded Caravan Map - Marked with old-world safe trails, trade routes, and burial caches. Slight boost to event discovery while traveling across open terrain. Barterable for a relic compass or strange herbal tonic (boosts healing once).

## Regional Landmarks and Remnants

### The Salt Catacombs of Tunisia

A subterranean network of ancient tombs fused with natural salt formations. Its air burns the lungs, but fungal growth is minimal. Rumored to house a rogue archivist enclave.

### The Glass Wadi of the Western Sahara

A dried riverbed turned obsidian mirror after a meteor strike during the early Bloom. The ground is cracked and glossy. At night, it reflects the stars... and sometimes, things that aren't.

### The Crooked Minaret of Sudan

A ruined spire that bends unnaturally westward. It has no known foundations, but broadcasts low-frequency Hum pulses every night at 3:14. Scavengers call it "the prayer that calls back."

## Old World Timeline Highlights

### 2020 – The Caravan Fall

A string of relief convoys traveling from Libya to Chad vanished en route across the Tenere Desert. Their last radio transmission described "a moving fog with bones." No bodies, no wreckage, only buried wheels and sun-bleached supplies remained.

### 2021 – Nile Quarantine Breach

Despite heavy containment, the infection breached Cairo's Nile corridor. Floating colonies and trade barges spread spores up and down the river. Some settlements along its banks still hang laundry across the water, unaware what breathes beneath it.

### 2022 – Red Storm over Marrakesh

A blood-colored sandstorm engulfed the city for three days. Survivors claim it cleared the Blightspores—but also erased entire districts. When the storm passed, maps no longer matched the streets.

## Historic Cities

Northern Africa's cities are as old as myth—built upon myth—and as vulnerable to decay. When the Bloom came, it didn't strike all at once. It moved through trade, breath, belief. Cities like Marrakesh, Cairo, and Tripoli stood as strongholds of identity, even as their walls buckled and their stories warped. Now they persist only in fragments: half-memory, half-spore, half-hope.

## Cairo Egypt

**Cairo**, once a labyrinth of life along the Nile, became a city of tombs once more. The sprawling metropolis was overwhelmed by the infected early on, its ancient streets now echoing with the whispers of the dead. Gravebloomer cults have taken root among the relics of the old dynasties, and scavengers willing to brave the ruins speak of strange, fungal-covered effigies honoring forgotten pharaohs.

## Algiers, Algeria

**Algiers**' whitewashed buildings once gleamed over the Mediterranean Sea. Now, they are weathered and broken, worn by time and plague. The port city initially attempted isolation, blockading its harbors and fortifying its hills, but the Necromyce found a way through desperate hands. Today, Algiers is a bitter city of fragmented factions, its steep alleys home to smugglers, mercenaries, and a few stubborn dreamers still clinging to civilization.

## Tripoli, Libya

Already scarred by years of unrest, **Tripoli** was ill-prepared when the infection surged ashore. What order remained collapsed swiftly into chaos. In the ruins of crumbling mosques and gutted apartment blocks, Gravebloomers and survivors alike struggle for control, and ghost stories tell of hidden sanctuaries deep within the old Roman ruins—if one is desperate enough to search for them.

## Marrakesh, Morocco

The Red City of **Marrakesh**, famed for its vibrant markets and labyrinthine alleys, became a fortress of defiance. For a time, the old medina walls held back the infected, offering one of the last bastions of hope in North Africa. But hope is a brittle thing. Now, Marrakesh stands as a patchwork of fortified compounds and quarantined ruins, where spices, stories, and suspicion all trade at the same price.

## Lagos, Nigeria

Once Africa's most populous city, **Lagos** was a humming titan of industry, trade, and culture before the Fall. When the infection spread, the city's dense population turned from its greatest strength to its gravest weakness. Entire districts fell overnight, and the mainland and island sectors became battlegrounds. Now, pockets of survivors barter from crumbling skyscrapers and decaying shipping yards, while Gravebloomers are rumored to have established a formidable "Bloom Court" deep within the city's overgrown outskirts.
