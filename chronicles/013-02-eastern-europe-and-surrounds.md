# Eastern Europe and Surrounds

Long a crossroads of empires and battles, **Eastern Europe** bore the scars of old wars and the dreams of new beginnings. Its ancient cities, cobblestone alleys, and fortified citadels provided some of the fiercest early resistance against the Necromyce. Yet pride could not hold back decay. Governments fractured, alliances shattered, and the streets that once sang with revolution fell silent beneath fungal vines.

The Carpathians breathe steam again. Not from dragons—but reactors, buried and still hot under the frost.

## Natural Disasters

Earthquakes, floods and extreme winters.

## Minerals and Resources

Coal, uranium, rare forests and black soil for agriculture.

## Survival Insight

Underground bunkers in some zones may have been reactivated and others' caves became shelters.

## Stubborn lineage. Resistance blood.

Starting in Eastern Europe grants increased resilience to cold and a cultural instinct for hidden paths—bunkers, tunnels, ruins long forgotten. You begin with minor morale protection during isolation or loss, and a faint edge in spotting ruins from older wars.

## Starting Inventory

🪓 Hand-Forged Hatchet - Passed down since before the Collapse. Excellent for crafting shelters and melee defense. Can be traded for 15 materials or an encrypted radio shard (useless until decoded).

## Regional Landmarks and Remnants

### The Black Tunnel of Ukraine

A derelict metro line where light bends unnaturally. Scouts report growing fungal matter that doesn’t reflect in mirrors.

### The Vlach Cradle of Transylvania

A mountain fissure where blood-red spores burst seasonally. Locals say the earth "breathes different" here.

### The Smolensk Static Wall

A stretch of abandoned telecom towers crackling with random noise bursts. Fragment decoders believe it’s not random at all.

## Old World Timeline Highlights

### 2020 – Reactor Quarantine Failures

A string of aging power facilities in Ukraine, Romania, and Bulgaria failed containment checks due to staff shortages and coordination collapse. Containment zones were breached by desperate civilians within weeks.

### 2021 – The Balkan Evacuation Gridlock

A failed multi-border evacuation across Serbia, Croatia, and Hungary caused the largest civilian bottleneck in the region’s modern history. Thousands vanished into the forests or flooded bunkers never meant to be reopened.

### 2022 – Operation Black Frost

Russian winter operations against “unclassified biozones” ended in silence. Satellite footage showed entire platoons frozen mid-stride. No heat signatures ever returned.

## Historic Cities

In Eastern Europe, cities were more than settlements—they were memory palaces. Their walls remembered every war, revolution, and hymn. When the Fall came, many of these cities resisted with a kind of inherited defiance. But not even centuries of struggle could withstand Necromyce. Now, their spires and squares wear fungal cloaks, and the old songs echo through frostbitten air as ghosts of culture lost.

### Warsaw, Poland

**Warsaw** stood proud, rebuilt from the ashes of past wars. Its people, too stubborn to break, fought the infection street by street. For a while, it worked. Resistance bands roamed the Old Town's alleys like phantoms. But even Warsaw's spirit could not halt the inevitable. Today, fragments of patriot songs drift down deserted boulevards lined with crumbling monuments.

### Bucharest, Romania

**Bucharest**, a city of contradictions—grand palaces beside brutalist towers—Bucharest was a labyrinth of history. When the Necromyce bloom unfurled across its streets, survival twisted into something colder. Gangs now rule the abandoned districts, trading scraps of ancient technology for water and medicine. Somewhere beneath the crumbling Parliament Palace, legends say a Gravebloomer enclave stirs.

### Prague, Czech Republic

The City of a Hundred Spires gleamed once with romance, art, and gothic grandeur. Its stone bridges and castle walls withstood centuries of storms and sieges. But not this one. **Prague** today is a city of shadows: ivy-cloaked cathedrals, silent squares, and statues weeping fungal tears as the Vltava River quietly reclaims its banks.

### Budapest, Hungary

Twin cities once divided by the Danube, **Budapest** was a jewel of Europe, a bridge between past and future. When collapse came, it came fast. The underground thermal baths—once a source of healing—became fetid breeding grounds for spores. Survivors whisper of strange things rising with the steam, cloaked in rot and sorrow.

### Moscow, Russia

The iron heart of Russia, **Moscow**'s great monuments—the Kremlin, Red Square—are now crumbling bastions draped in fungal growth. Beneath the ice and rot, hidden bunkers shelter those few who refuse to abandon the motherland. The Blightspore presence here is relentless; their Hum reverberates through the underground metro tunnels, making each expedition above or below ground a gamble. Yet still, in the heart of winter, the spirit of resistance lingers like frost on broken windows.

### St. Petersburg, Russia

Once called Russia's window to the West, **St. Petersburg**'s elegant canals and palaces have drowned under both floodwaters and creeping death. The Necromyce infection bloomed wildly here, accelerated by the wet climate. Survivors cling to fortified islands, trading through risky boat crossings. Every tide could bring salvation—or a drifting horror pulled along by unseen currents. Legends tell of an ancient vault hidden beneath the Hermitage Museum, rumored to hold relics of the old world untouched by plague.
