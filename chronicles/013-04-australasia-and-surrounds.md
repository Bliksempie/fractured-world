# Australasia and Surrounds

The southern lands—the sun-drenched coasts of **Australia and the green mountains of New Zealand**—believed distance and isolation would save them. For a time, it did. Refugees flooded in from the dying north. But Necromyce was patient. It traveled unseen in cargo holds and infected flesh. When it finally bloomed in the southern hemisphere, it consumed paradise with the same ruthless hunger, leaving cities silent under endless blue skies.

The soil here never cooled. Not since the ancestors walked barefoot on the lava fields. Some say the heat never left their bones.

## Natural Disasters

Wildfires, cyclones, droughts and earthquakes.

## Minerals and Resources

Bauxite, gold and geothermal.

## Survival Insight

Some tribes may have returned to pre-colonial survival methods, some's geothermal zones allow heated shelters.

## Bushcraft and wind-worn patience.

Players who begin in Australasia gain a knack for environmental adaptation—making the most of sparse shelter and water. Slightly improved trap-building and fire-making skills, and a reduced penalty when navigating storms or tropical decay zones.

## Starting Inventory

🪢 Field Repair Kit - Compact bundle with wire, tape, fasteners, and bushcraft tools. Boosts crafting success slightly. Trade value: 12 parts or 1 random weapon upgrade component.

## Regional Landmarks and Remnants

### The Nullarbor Scar

Once the flattest road-stretch in the world. Now home to a 100-kilometer fungal trench. Glows faintly at night. Avoid during full moons—patterns form in the dirt.

### Rotorua's Hollow Bones of New Zealand

Geothermal village where ancient structures still stand, but no wildlife enters. Locals say the heat smells wrong, and sound disappears inside the crater walls.

### The Singing Reef of Micronesia

A submerged ruin marked by coral blooms in symmetrical patterns. Divers report hearing low, harmonic pulses through the water. One called it "a Gravebloomer lullaby."

## Old World Timeline Highlights

### 2020 – The Darwin Reversal

A wave of northern Australian refugees were turned away at gunpoint by state militias attempting to preserve isolated southern zones. Many were forced into the interior deserts, where contact was soon lost.

### 2021 – Tasman Exodus

New Zealand, overwhelmed by incoming refugees from the Pacific Rim, closed all ports. Civilian boats were turned away or sunk. Coastal cities rioted; smaller islands went dark and were never heard from again.

### 2022 – The Ashen Rainfall

A massive volcanic eruption near Vanuatu coincided with the first recorded Necromyce bloom on the southern ocean's edge. Survivors reported red ash, coughing spores, and the first known aquatic mutation of Gravebloomers.

## Historic Cities

In Australasia, cities were bastions of calm and hope—until they weren't. Their collapse came not in fire, but in suffocating silence. Built to resist heat and distance, they succumbed to scarcity and the creeping infection. Now, their towers watch over empty harbors, and their streets crack beneath vines, salt, and memory. Few places held on as long—or fell so completely.

### Sydney, Australia

The shining jewel of Australia's coast, **Sydney** fell in waves of panic and betrayal. The once-bustling harbor is now choked with rotting ships, their skeletal remains jutting from poisoned waters. Fungal blooms crawl up the steel bones of the Opera House and the Harbour Bridge, and the old suburbs are deadly labyrinths ruled by savage scavenger clans and Blightspore packs that hunt at dusk.

### Melbourne, Australia

**Melbourne**'s sprawling art districts and iconic laneways became grim mausoleums almost overnight. An early failed quarantine effort turned whole boroughs into festering nests of infection. The survivors that remain here live in isolated towers, their windows shuttered against the toxic winds that sweep down from the Yarra River. Amid the decay, underground markets thrive—trading knowledge, old tech, and dangerous hope.

### Auckland, New Zealand

**Auckland**, the "City of Sails," once hoped to ride out the apocalypse behind its island borders. It was a fleeting dream. Now its shores are silent, its marinas rotting and its glass towers shattered by nature's slow but steady hand. In the thick, misty hills surrounding the city, communities fight not just against the infected, but against each other, old rivalries flaring in the face of endless desperation.

### Port Moresby, Papua New Guinea

**Port Moresby** became a paradox of survival. Rugged mountains shielded its people longer than most, but when the infection breached their defenses, it spread like wildfire through the dense settlements. Now, the city's overgrown streets and jungled outskirts are home to fierce tribal enclaves—some fighting to preserve old ways, others embracing the chaos with terrifying new traditions born of plague and despair.

### Suva, Fiji

Once a vibrant hub of Pacific trade and tropical tourism, **Suva** stood proudly as the heart of Fiji's culture and commerce. Its colorful markets, colonial architecture, and sprawling harbor bustled with life. When the Necromyce Bloom reached these shores, isolation bought Suva a few precious months of uneasy peace. But the infection spread relentlessly across the islands, leaving the once-celebrated capital eerily silent. Now, Suva's jungle-choked streets hide both the remnants of island survivors and strange pockets of Gravebloomer settlements that worship the infection as divine retribution. The ocean, once a lifeline, has become another boundary few dare to cross.
