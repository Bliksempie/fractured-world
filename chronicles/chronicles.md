# The Living Chronicles

"This is not a book. It's a wound stitched shut with ink and memory."
— Unknown Archivist, Ironroot Vault

The Living Chronicles are not static. They shift. They breathe. They remember. What one survivor records as truth, another may never encounter. Some fragments are echoes. Some are lies. Others—whispers of a deeper truth, waiting to be unearthed.

The world you walk through is yours, and so too is the Chronicle it leaves behind. No two survivors carry the same pages. No two fates align the same way.

⚠️ **Be warned:** A truth confirmed by another does not make it so for you. This record reflects your discoveries, your scars, and your unraveling of the fractured world. Nothing more. **Read carefully. Trust cautiously. Chronicle relentlessly.**

## The Fractured World

You are living in roughly Cycle 78—seventy-eight years after the original Necromyce outbreak.

**Only two known calendars remain:**

- **Cycles** — used by **The Uninfected**, this system replaced the old-world year and tracks time in natural increments: seasons, moon shifts, and survival milestones.
- **Sheddings** — used by **The Gravebloomers**, this calendar is generational, marking each bloodline that survives and mutates beyond the last. Some colonies measure time from their first Shedding, others from a pivotal Bloodroot Rite. Higher Sheddings are said to be purer—cleansed through ritual and sacrifice, believed to bring them closer to a perfected form of the bloom.

_The world ended not with fire or war, but with a whisper carried through the crust of the earth_. The **Necromyce infection** bloomed from the ground itself—unstoppable, inevitable. In mere months, cities crumbled under waves of fungus-choked bodies.

First, the earth could not be trusted anymore. Water. Vegetation. Animals. All of them spread the infection without being directly affected. Roads collapsed. Communications fell silent. Governments toppled. Humanity shattered.

Even the original Necromyce strain eventually burned itself out—but not before it evolved into two horrors even more profound:

The first became known as the **Blightspores**—creatures feral and monstrous, lost to madness within hours of infection. They did not live long enough to age. They fought, they killed, they died. The eerie sound they emitted—the **Hum**—bound them into brutal, mindless swarms. Regional variations of the Hum distinguished one colony from another, but anything that did not echo their dialect was torn apart without mercy—even other Blightspore colonies.

The second strain, the **Gravebloomers**, clung stubbornly to fragments of humanity. They could think. They could plan. They could collaborate with other Gravebloomer colonies—or even uninfected, when it suited their purposes. They could hate. Remembering what they once were, the Gravebloomers grew bitter toward the uninfected, seeing them not as survivors, but as thieves who stole the life they had lost. They aged. They procreated. And as each new generation forgot a little more of what it meant to be human, their resentment curdled into twisted lore, carried along from generation to generation and colony to colony.

The uninfected now cling to survival in scattered colonies across the wastelands. Food is scarce. Clean water is a dream. Trust is scarce, and a currency few can afford.

Every decision—every step you take—will shape not only your fate but the future of those around you.

There is **no cure**. There are **no saviors**. There is only the **fractured world**—and what you choose to build from the ashes.

## 🜏 The Gravity of the Soul
> "In the end, the world does not ask what you knew. Only what you chose."
> _— Fragment from The Testament of the Tainted_

Some believe that every action in the fractured world carries a weight.

A choice to burn a field. Spare a mimic. Lie to a survivor. Feed a child. Trade hope for comfort. These actions don't vanish — they gather. Beneath the surface. Behind the eyes.

Survivors call it **The Gravity** — an unseen pull within the soul. Some say it is Necromyce itself, whispering through the marrow. Others say it is what's left of the world trying to balance itself.

**And sometimes, in the quiet hours near the end, a voice comes.**

- It does not call itself good or evil.
- It only reflects what you've earned.

It may plead. It may condemn. It may tell you the world is worth saving. Or that nothing pure remains. But in the final moment — you decide. And the Gravity watches.

Fungal Strain / Threat

These are the first mutated strain. Blightspores are feral, and were thought to be mindless and chaotic. And yet... something binds them. The Hum. A droning frequency that connects, organizes, and controls them—sometimes with eerie precision.

They emit piercing cries when they spot prey—triggering others nearby to join the hunt. One shriek in the distance can mean a dozen claws at your throat in minutes. Silence isn't just golden—it's survival.

### Skulkers

Over time, the infection warps their instincts to something darker. **Skulkers** lurk in shadows, blending into ruined landscapes, and striking only when prey is vulnerable. Their breathing is ragged but their patience is lethal. You never hear a Skulker coming. It waits in the black, molding itself to the silence. When you hear it, you better have fast reflexes.

Their distorted cries are bone-chilling—half anger, half hunger. The first warning is a sound, not a sight: a series of sickening cracks, like stone splitting under heat. If you hear it, run. Massive fluid-filled sacs develop along the body, swelling under the skin like tumors stretched to bursting. These sacs are not passive—they are biological pressure bombs. When ruptured (whether by injury or decay), they release a violent burst of infectious spores and toxic biological matter, capable of infecting anyone caught in the blast radius.

#### Radiation Zones and the Blisterskins (a.k.a. Bubblers)

When the Necromyce Bloom shattered civilization, it wasn't just the living that were twisted. The old world's scars—broken nuclear plants, devastated reactors, ghost cities of fallout—festered unattended. Over time, certain pockets of lingering radiation became unnatural cradles for new horrors.

Within these zones, a rare mutation sometimes occurred among Blightspores. They did not merely decay or die—they adapted.

These creatures, now called Blisterskins (or Bubblers by those who dare speak of them), are grotesque evolutions of their kin. Their skin crusts over with swollen, pustulant growths—living tumors that pulse with toxic decay. They hum not in the voice of the Hum, but in a broken, sluggish echo, a sound that does not tie them to their own kind.

Blisterskins or Bubblers cannot leave their radiation nests.

Legends say that the poisoned blood binding them unravels the moment they step too far from the irradiated earth. Few who have seen a Bubbler cross a dead zone's edge have lived to tell of the way they collapse into shrieking heaps of ash and bile.

Yet their presence strengthens the contamination. A Bubbler's death too near its home can cause the radiation to spread, corrupting new ground, growing the dead zone wider, stronger. Only through cleverness—luring them far and killing them cleanly—can a survivor hope to reclaim these cursed spaces.

Still, rumors persist that hidden wonders remain buried in these dead lands. Secrets. Salvage. Salvation.

Those brave or desperate enough to breach a radiation zone must balance their greed against the terrible cost of failure. In Fractured World, every scar on the earth still bleeds. Some wounds were never meant to heal.

### Titans

Few Blightspores survive long enough to reach this terrible final state. **Titans** are mountains of rot and muscle, layers of hardened spore-matter encasing what little remains of the host. Slow but unstoppable, they smash through barricades, walls, and defenses alike. They don't run. They don't need to. A Titan's footfall can crack pavement—and your bones if you're foolish enough to stand in its path.

**Speed:** ⚡
**Strength:** ⚡⚡⚡⚡⚡
**Stealth:** None
**Threat Level:** Catastrophic

### Bloomspawn

Where Titans are mountains of corrupted muscle, Bloomspawn are something worse: intent given form. A Bloomspawn is not a single infected body—it is a fused abomination, a grotesque colony of Blightspore hosts knotted together by fungal growth, nerve, and blind instinct. No two Bloomspawn are exactly alike. Some crawl. Some slither. Some walk on fused limbs like broken gods. All of them spread Necromyce spores in their wake, leaving landscapes where nothing human survives. To face a Bloomspawn is not to fight. It is to choose between escape... or oblivion.

**Speed:** ⚡⚡
**Strength:** ⚡⚡⚡⚡⚡
**Stealth:** ⚡
**Threat Level:** Apocalyptic

## Rumours

### The Black Choir

Among survivors and even whispering Gravebloomer elders, there is talk of **The Black Choir**—a supposed Blightspore phenomenon where entire hordes move in eerie, perfect unison, not merely through instinct, but through something deeper. A pure, brutal harmony of mindless will.

The few who supposedly survived long enough to speak—described it as follows: **the Hum** rising into a deafening, coordinated wail, strong enough to shatter windows, rupture eardrums, and drive animals mad. Blightspores under its spell move with unnatural precision: stopping, pivoting, even forming near-military patterns to encircle prey.

An unnatural silence afterward, leaving entire villages abandoned without a trace of struggle—as if The Black Choir simply swept them away. Some scholars (and a few mad Gravebloomer exiles) claim that if enough Blightspores gather together, and under the right circumstances, something inside their infected blood "awakens," briefly linking them into a temporary hive mind far more dangerous than their normal animalistic state.

No one agrees whether The Black Choir is a true evolution of the Blightspores—or simply the last, horrific illusion before death.

Other rumors about The Black Choir have been heard:

- Choirstones: Crumbled ruins found at supposed Black Choir incident sites, where strange fungal growths vibrate with faint echoes of long-past Hums.
- The Dissonance: Rare survivors speak of trying to counter a Black Choir by disrupting their Hum with loud noise or conflicting signals—a near-suicidal act.
- Silent Choirs: In extremely rare cases, Blightspores have been seen moving without any sound at all, coordinated entirely by some unseen force.

### The Blight King

Among hushed voices around dying campfires, there persists a terrifying legend—one so blasphemous that even most Gravebloomers refuse to acknowledge it: "There was once a **Blight King**—a Blightspore who did not lose his mind."

Unlike the shrieking hordes bound by instinct and Hum, the Blight King was said to retain a brutal form of intelligence. Not human kindness. Not reason. Pure malevolent cunning fused with the raw, infectious hunger of the Blight.

He rose during the early days, when Necromyce's children were still chaotic, when colonies clashed and devoured one another without thought. The Blight King united **the Hum** of dozens of disparate colonies into a single terrible symphony, orchestrating mass assaults on survivor settlements. Entire regions were swallowed into silence overnight. Gravebloomer camps were razed, their memories burned to ash.

They say he built a kingdom in the ruins—a place where Blightspores marched in disciplined phalanxes, where the Hum sang strategies, not just madness. But how did it end? Nobody knows.

**Stories are few and varying:**

- Some claim his kingdom consumed itself, the sheer mass of Hum creating a psychic collapse that shredded every mind at once.
- Others believe the Gravebloomers rallied in secret and unleashed a blood ritual so devastating that even the Blight King could not endure it.
- A few whisper he was never truly defeated, only slumbering somewhere deep beneath the earth, his corrupted mind waiting to rise again if the Blight grows strong enough...

**Other rumors that have been heard are:**

- **The Throne of Spines:** Said to still exist somewhere in the deep ruins—a towering fungal growth shaped like a twisted throne, humming so low it rattles teeth.
- **The Discord:** Some surviving Blight colonies show strange "anomalies" in their Hum, as if echoes of the Blight King's unified song still linger in their blood.
- **Dreams of the Crown:** Very rarely, Gravebloomer Bishops are driven mad by sudden dreams of a fungal crown calling to them.

### The Last King of Blight

Some say he was the first of the Bubblers. Others whisper he was the last man who dared rule over the infected, a tyrant who wore the blight like armor and waged war across the poisoned zones. No one agrees on the truth.

According to wandering scholars, there was once a figure known as The Last King of Blight who emerged from the fallout, neither fully Gravebloomer nor Blightspore—something different, something terrible.

It's said he learned to move freely between radiation zones, using the poisoned earth itself to heal and command legions of the deformed. Survivors tell tales of banners stitched from the skins of both Blightspores and men, flying above shattered strongholds before they were swallowed again by death and silence.

Whether The Last King of Blight still roams, or whether he ever lived at all, none can say. But in the darkest corners of abandoned colonies, his legend stirs the embers of both fear and hope: that even monsters might be kings... and kings, monsters.
# The Gravebloomers

## Type

Fungal Strain / Emergent Species

## Description

The second known surviving strain of Necromyce. Once considered superstition, Gravebloomers evolved in silence—first scavengers, then thinkers, then warlords. They age, reproduce, and remember. Their memories define time, not the sun or the stars.

These victims of the Necromyce retained intelligence and memory, are organized into colonies, capable of speech, aging, and reproduction.

## First Recorded

Approximately 4 months after initial Necromyce outbreak.

## Date Reference

3rd Shedding, Cycle 9.2. A Shedding marks the birth of a new generation—but generational order is not strictly linear. Some fourth-generation Gravebloomers may be born before others in the third. Each colony tracks time its own way, using shared blood-memory and local rites.

### Date Example

3rd generation since awakening, 9th year, 2nd month.

## Known Facts

- Collaborate with their own kind; may occasionally cooperate with uninfected.
- Grow increasingly unstable across generations.
- View uninfected as "thieves" who stole their rightful humanity.

## Unconfirmed Myths or Rumors

- Some Gravebloomer colonies practice dark rituals in an attempt to "purify" their bloodline, believe to be called **The Bloodroot Rites**.
- They maintain an archive similar to **The Living Chronicles** and it is believed to be called **The Shroud Memory**.

## Variants

### Mimics

**Mimics** are fresh-born or newly infected Gravebloomers who mirror behavior they observe, especially from **Bishops** and **Kingpins**. They form cult-like mobs, dangerous only in numbers. A single Mimic might whimper and retreat. A dozen? They'll tear you apart with a smile they learned from watching others do the same.

**Speed:** ⚡⚡⚡
**Strength:** ⚡
**Stealth:** ⚡
**Threat Level:** Low on their own, but devastating under group mind control by Bishops or Kingpins.

### Wretches

**Wretches** are Gravebloomers that have failed to become Bishops—driven mad by their inability to rise in the colony's hierarchy. Fast, twitchy, and unstable, they act as scouts and saboteurs, prowling the edges of Uninfected territories. Their minds are sharp but fractured—able to mimic strategic thinking but prone to sudden, violent rages. Alone, a Wretch is a manageable threat. In numbers, they can outmaneuver and cripple entire survivor enclaves before Bishops move in to claim the ruins.

**Speed:** ⚡⚡⚡⚡
**Strength:** ⚡
**Stealth:** ⚡⚡⚡⚡
**Threat Level:** Medium (high if undetected)

### Bishops

These Gravebloomers are the field commanders of the their colonies. Ruthless and charismatic, they maintain the cohesion of Mimics through loyalty and fear. They have been in service of the Gravebloomers for some time, and have earned their position of power. A Bishop's voice carries weight, even in the carnage of battle. When they call, the hordes of Mimics does not hesitate—they obey.

**Speed:** ⚡⚡⚡
**Strength:** ⚡⚡
**Stealth:** ⚡⚡⚡
**Threat Level:** Medium (always surrounded by tens of minions)

### Tenders

**Tenders** are grotesque Gravebloomers who have fused partially with their own fungal structures. Assigned to "grow"' colonies deeper into the earth, they drag heavy organic growths behind them like decaying gardeners. Their breath spores seed new Mimics in sleeping humans, and their bodies act as slow-moving nurseries for fresh infections. A single Tender left unchecked will turn an entire region into a spawning ground within months. They do not speak. They do not fight in the traditional sense. They infect the world itself.

**Speed:** ⚡
**Strength:** ⚡⚡⚡
**Stealth:** ⚡ (none once active)
**Threat Level:** Catastrophic (over time, not immediately)

### Kingpins

These are the oldest and most mentally resilient Gravebloomers. Weak in body but powerful in will, they anchor the colony's memory, myths, and vendettas through subtle psychic influence passed down through generations. Among their kind, they preserve their own version of **The Living Chronicles**—a lore never written, never spoken aloud, and known only through whispered transmissions of thought. No Uninfected has ever seen evidence of it, or even knows what the Gravebloomers call it, but rumours exist of a journal called **The Shroud Memory**. When a Kingpin hums its bitter song into the minds of its followers, even death itself seems afraid to step too near.

**Speed:** ⚡
**Strength:** ⚡
**Stealth:** None
**Threat Level:** Apocalyptic (always surrounded by hundreds of minions)

## Rumours

### The Bloodroot Rites

In the deeper wastelands, survivors speak of **The Bloodroot Rites**—grim rituals performed by Gravebloomer colonies seeking to "purify" their bloodline. The goal, it is said, is not to reclaim their lost humanity, but to sever the last fragile ties to it.

Through grotesque ceremonies involving the ingestion of rare fungal blooms, ritual mutilation, or violent rites of passage, they believe they can strengthen their minds against decay... or accelerate their evolution into something "purer" than either human or Gravebloomer.

**Stories vary:**

- In some tellings, entire colonies are consumed by madness after the Rites, reduced to husks worse than Blightspores.
- In others, rare individuals survive, becoming twisted savants—revered, feared, and utterly alien.
- Survivors claim some Gravebloomers bear scars or fungal tattoos from undergoing the Rites. These are known as Bloodmarked Gravebloomers.
- Although not heard often, there are rumors of Bloom Ascendants, rare evolved leaders resulting from a successful Bloodroot Rite. Rumored to be stronger than its root variant, and might be able to mutate.
- Perhaps scarier than any of the other rumors if true, is the one of Failed Rites. There are talks of whole colonies of Gravebloomers that are stronger than any Gravebloomer ever seen before—as strong as a Bishop physically, but needs more assistance from a Kingpin to control his minions.
It remains uncertain whether these Bloodroot Rites are real, exaggerated by fear, or fabricated as warnings to the uninfected and Gravebloomers alike. But one thing is clear: when a Gravebloomer colony changes overnight, it's never good news for anyone nearby.

### The Shroud Memory

Among wandering scholars and in the broken whispers of survivor camps, there persists a tale—not of a place or a person, but of a living memory, carried deep within the Gravebloomers.

No written record has ever been found. No Gravebloomer has ever confirmed its existence. Yet still the rumors grow.

**It is called by many names:**

- The Shroud Memory.
- The Rooted Dream.
- The Murmuring Archive.
- The Crimson Bloom.

It is said that within this psychic web, Gravebloomers pass their history not through words or ink, but through something far more sinister—an inheritance embedded directly into flesh and mind. From generation to generation, a shared consciousness blooms—not perfect, but persistent.

- They remember the wrongs done to them.
- They remember the cities that burned and the faces that cursed their names.
- They remember grievances, not as cold history, but as bleeding, personal wounds.

Those born into Gravebloomer colonies—even second or third generations who never knew a human life—inherit these memories. They remember hate before they know language.

Some scholars believe **The Shroud Memory** is why Gravebloomers' resentment grows stronger with each passing year.

Others fear that somewhere deep within the Shroud, there might still survive the knowledge of the old world—technologies, weapons, horrors—forgotten by the uninfected but preserved in the echoing marrow of their enemies.

If the myth is true, then the Gravebloomers remember more than any Uninfected dares to hope... or fear., and the day they truly awaken those memories might be the day the last light of The Uninfected gutters out.
# The Uninfected

The Uninfected are simply humans untouched by the Necromyce infection. Some believe they are immune; others were simply lucky—or cautious enough to avoid exposure. In this world, revealing an infection is a death sentence. Even a suspected scratch marks you as a threat. Fear drives exile. Or worse.

In the Age Before the Bloom, they were simply called humans. Survivors. Families. Strangers. Now, they are known only as The Uninfected—a name born not from pride or superiority, but from necessity. When the world fractured, the old divisions of nation, race, creed, and faith collapsed into dust. All that mattered was survival, and a banner to unite under before the dark swallowed everything.

Among The Uninfected are the rare few whose bodies resisted Necromyce's grip. No one knows why. Some whisper of bloodlines, others of sheer luck. No cure has ever been proven, no true immunity ever confirmed. They are not heroes. They are not chosen. They are simply what remains.

Why not call themselves human? Because The Infected were human once, too. Keyword: **Were**.

Among the shattered colonies and wastes, The Uninfected live on borrowed time, hunted by Blightspores and Gravebloomers alike. Trust is rare. Hope, rarer still. They cling to the bones of a dead world, building what little they can atop its grave.

# Fragments of Wisdon

Fragments of Wisdom are rare, often cryptic relics scattered across both the **Last Light** campaign and the endless expanse of **Blinding Light**. They are echoes of a world undone—etched into bone, whispered through fungus, carved into rusted steel. No two players will gather the same fragments, and no two **Living Chronicles** will ever tell the same story.

While the **Living Chronicles** can be accessed at any time—halting direct threats to your companions—the world itself does not stop. Winds shift. Shadows grow. The bloom endures. And fragments? They do not shape the Chronicles until they are studied in safety.

But tread carefully. Not all fragments speak truth. Some are fevered mutterings of the broken. Others... deliberate lies, seeded by things that remember being human.

## The Testament of the Tainted

In the shattered world left behind by Necromyce, memory itself became a casualty. Stories twisted. Facts decayed. _Truths frayed into myth_.

Yet some remnants—fragments of forgotten knowledge, sacred rituals, bitter confessions—were salvaged by those who refused to let the past be wholly devoured.

These relics of wisdom are gathered into the Testament of the Tainted—a secret vault of humanity's broken memory, stitched together from blood, sorrow, and stubborn hope.

Each Fragment whispers a piece of what was... Or warns of what is yet to come. The Testament remembers. **Even when the world forgets**.

## Fragment Timing

- **Earlygame**: Fragments are mostly unconfirmed myths and scattered stories, but some may end up being true.

# ☍ Gravity Mechanic

## Gravity

Gravity is a hidden value adjusted throughout gameplay based on:

- Actions taken (e.g., mercy vs. ruthlessness)
- Fragments uncovered (e.g., hopeful lore vs. nihilistic revelations)
- Events experienced (e.g., survivor betrayals, discovered atrocities)

At the end, a "voice" appears — not a real person, but a manifestation of accumulated moral weight.

## Depending on the Gravity score

- Significantly below 0 -> the voice tempts, condemns, seduces: "End it. Let the bloom finish what it began."
- Significantly above 0 -> the voice encourages, questions, inspires: """There is still light. A future waits in blood and ash."
- Neutral (insignificantly above or below 0, nobody knows the exact threshold) -> the voice is uncertain: """Nothing is certain. Nothing is promised. But you still choose."

# Fragment Analysis

Fragments are found, not understood. Until they are brought to safety, they remain dormant — no more than riddles in glass or echoes etched in metal.

To analyze a fragment, the player must reach a Safe Zone and initiate the **Fragment Analysis** ritual. This may take time or resources, depending on the zone's type. Once analyzed:

- If the fragment is **mundane or broken**, it may add flavor or world-building detail, but not affect gameplay.
- If the fragment is **myth-bound**, it may shift the tone or content of the Living Chronicle, marking the event as myth or memory.
- If the fragment is **high-value** (often rare or region-specific), it may:
  - Alter player morality (Gravity).
  - Unlock new areas or survivors.
  - Trigger passive memory shifts, unlocking backlogged lore or spoken echoes.
  - Set up delayed or chained events.

Unanalyzed fragments may still carry faint atmospheric weight — slight warmth, hallucinations, or Hum interference — but they cannot shape the world until read under safety and silence.

Fragments are not inventory. They are *beliefs waiting for proof.*

# Safe Zones

Safe Zones are the only places where time stabilizes and the player can act without immediate consequence. There is no universal layout — some are crumbling shelters, others bio-sealed bunkers, even ancient stone circles humming with stillness.

Inside a Safe Zone:

- Players may **analyze fragments**, **rest**, **eat**, **repair**, or **commune** with echo traces.
- Certain zones allow **rituals**, **resonance tuning**, or access to long-forgotten **vaults**.
- Safe Zones also **reset Anomaly Pressure** and temporarily suppress Hum effects.
- If present, other survivors may offer trades, stories, or regional rumors (often linked to the player's Living Chronicle state).

Safe Zones are not *safe* in the traditional sense. They are stable — for now. Each one has a **Signal Stability Rating**, which decays over time or with overuse. Once unstable, its functions begin to falter.

Some Safe Zones move. Others appear only during certain Cycles.

Every survivor remembers their first Safe Zone. Fewer remember their last.

# Starting Regions

The place you woke defines more than just geography — it whispers through every choice you make. Starting Regions in *Fractured World* determine:

- **Opening Inventory** — a tool, relic, or map echoing that region's final days.
- **Passive Trait** — slight buffs unique to each location (e.g., resistance to mountain fatigue, stealth in fungal mist, crafting precision).
- **Subtle Dialogue Variants** — some survivors react differently based on your accent, scent, or inherited silence.

Each region has three known Trade Opportunities:
1. **Forsaken Cache** — bury your bonus, and walk away. The story might find you later.
2. **Salvage Vault** — exchange for items or fragments with mechanical value.
3. **Nomad Merchant** — barter for unpredictable, lore-rich gear.

These trades are **permanent**, and only one can be claimed. Once the choice is made, the others fade.

The region you started in will be remembered — by you, by the world, and by those who listen for your voice in the Hum.

# Environmental Gauges

You are more than just alive. You are breaking, surviving, forgetting, enduring — all at once. The world does not care about numbers. But *you* must.

The player is tracked by several environmental gauges. They are not always visible — only when relevant, fractured, or at the brink of collapse.

## Core Gauges:

- **Health** – Physical condition. Reduced by wounds, infection, or extreme cold. Restored by rest, food, or rare meds.
- **Hunger** – Starvation increases Fatigue and lowers Clarity. Prolonged hunger also causes hallucinations during fog cycles.
- **Fatigue** – Influences action success. High fatigue causes failed crafts, poor reaction time, or narrative instability.
- **Mental Clarity** – Determines resistance to Hum, hallucinations, and false fragments. Drops in cursed zones or after betrayal.
- **Morale** – Not joy. Not hope. Just the *will* to keep moving. Boosted by story events, survivor presence, small comforts, and food rituals.

Each gauge interacts with others. Hunger causes Fatigue. Low Clarity may distort Morale. Damage to one is rarely isolated.

Most gauges regenerate slowly in Safe Zones, but only fully recover through rituals, kindness, or choices that align with your Chronicle.

Players may temporarily suppress gauge effects — through stimulants, vows, or psychological techniques — but never without cost.

You are not a hero. You are a *barometer of collapse*. Keep your dials in the green... or redefine what green means.

# Inventory System

What you carry defines what you survive. And what you survive... defines what you become.

The player's inventory is organized into six core categories. Each is limited — not just by space, but by *tolerance*. The world only lets you carry so much before it starts asking questions.

## Inventory Categories:

### Weapons

- Primary and secondary tools of defense or aggression. Includes melee and ranged.

### Ammunition

Stored separately from weapons. Must be loaded manually when depleted.

### Gear

Survival tools, protective items, and tactical equipment.

### Loot

Salvaged or quest-critical items. Often fragmented, encrypted, or damaged.

### Parts

Raw components used for repair, upgrades, and craftable traps or tech.

### Materials

Organic or elemental matter used in chemistry, patchwork, or healing.

## Inventory Loadout Limits

Each category has a default carry cap, which may expand through upgrades, traits, or tradeoffs:

### Weapons

- Two ranged
- One melee

### Ammunition

- Pistols and revolvers: 24
- Hunting rifles and shotguns: 16
- Sub-machine guns and Assault Rifles: 80
- Flamethrowers: 150

### Gear

- Heavier gear speeds of fatigue or increases noise
- Maximum 3 items without penalty.

### Loot

- Certain items may conflict

### Parts & Materials

- Stackable but weight adds up
- High quantities may attract certain NPCs or events

### Fragments

Fragments do not use inventory slots — they use *space in the story*.

> Some items *carry you*, whether you want them to or not.

# Echo Traces

Not all memories are yours. Some cling to the earth like frost. Others arrive uninvited, whispering names you never said.

**Echo Traces** are narrative impressions left behind in places marked by trauma, decision, or resonance. They are not visible on the map, nor consistent across playthroughs. They are *felt.*

## Key Features

- Echo Traces **spawn randomly**, up to 2 times per in-game day (24-hour cycle).
- Traces may occur:
  - Near significant structures (vaults, safe zones, ruins)
  - Following major player actions (e.g., analyzing a rare fragment, crossing Gravity thresholds)
  - During environmental events (fog shifts, Hum spikes, fragment alignments)

## When Discovered

- Players may "Listen" to the Echo, revealing:
  - **Survivor quotes** (raw, often unfinished)
  - **Flashback-style visions** (brief, lore-rich, never fully explained)
  - **Fragment echoes** (indirect lore reinforcement)

## Mechanical Effect

- Echo Traces do **not** directly alter gameplay systems.
- However, each echo subtly influences **Gravity**, especially if the player engages empathetically.
- Some may alter or amplify future **Safe Zone dialogue**, **dream sequences**, or **Living Chronicle tone**.

## Optional Responses

Players may:

- Dismiss the echo
- Record it in their Chronicle
- Share it with another survivor (if one is present)

Each choice *shapes perception* — of the world, of the past, and of who you might become.

The world is watching. And sometimes, it remembers *for* you.

# The Hum

It is not a sound. It is a presence. A pressure behind the ear. A song beneath your thoughts. Survivors call it **The Hum** — and none who hear it remain unchanged.

The Hum is an ever-present environmental force. It has no source, yet it resonates through stone, bone, and silence. It is tied to the infection, the collapse, and the fragments left behind.

## Hum Frequencies and Effects:

The Hum fluctuates in intensity and pitch. Players do not "see" it — they feel its effects.

- **Low-frequency Hum**:
  - Reduces **Morale** and suppresses survivor communication.
  - May trigger echo fragments from trauma zones.

- **Mid-frequency Hum**:
  - Interferes with **Fatigue** recovery and increases fragment distortion.
  - Causes spatial disorientation or "map ghosts" in ruined zones.

- **High-frequency Hum**:
  - Reduces **Mental Clarity** and makes fake fragments appear plausible.
  - Can trigger whispered hallucinations or false survivor memories.

## Hum Events

Random or region-triggered **Hum Events** may:

- Alter the tone or availability of survivor dialogue.
- Disrupt fragment analysis if not protected.
- Temporarily shift Gravity thresholds, forcing emotional reactions in otherwise neutral decisions.

## Damping the Hum

Survivors may protect themselves — imperfectly — through:

- **Signal-dampening helmets or filter masks**
- **Portable jammers** with limited battery life
- **Ritual actions** (humming, sketching, sleep phrasing) known only to a few

Even then, the Hum listens back.

> _"We didn't create the Hum. We simply got quiet enough to hear it."_

# Rest

Sleep is not safe. But it is necessary.

**Rest** is a core survival action, used to recover **Fatigue**, restore minor **Health**, and stabilize **Morale**. It is the only reliable way to prevent spiral effects from prolonged exhaustion, hunger, or clarity loss.

## Rest Types

- **Short Rest** (Anywhere Safe or Cleared)
  - Partial recovery of Fatigue and Morale.
  - Can be interrupted by nearby Hum fluctuations or environmental anomalies.
  - May trigger **fragment echoes** or half-dreams (non-Chronicle events).

- **Full Sleep** (Safe Zones only)
  - Full recovery of Fatigue, significant boost to Morale and minor Health gain.
  - Has a chance to trigger:
    - **Dream Fragments** — randomized lore hallucinations influenced by Gravity.
    - **Chronicle Flashbacks** — narrative replays of past events the player has forgotten or suppressed.

# Scout

The world does not reveal itself. It must be **scouted** — foot by foot, breath by breath.

**Scout** allows players to explore nearby regions, discover unknown paths, hidden caches, and potential events. It carries moderate risk, but is essential to avoid ambushes, unlock new areas, and uncover buried lore.

## Mechanics

- Costs moderate **Fatigue**, small **Clarity**
- Available outside Safe Zones only
- Influenced by:
  - Region difficulty
  - Gear equipped (e.g., working compass, filter mask)
  - Time of day or Cycle phase

## Possible Outcomes

- **Pathfinding** – Uncovers nearby structures, fragments, danger zones
- **Event Detection** – Reveals creature signs, safe houses, faint Hum anomalies
- **Echo Discovery** – May trigger a dormant Echo Trace (if spawn limit not reached)
- **Ambush Avoidance** – Prevents narrative or combat traps

## Risk Factors

- Low Clarity or broken equipment increases chance of:
  - Misdirection (false location markers)
  - Environmental harm (thorns, spores, drops)
  - Minor negative Gravity shift (paranoia-induced choices)

> You scout to see the road. But often, you find yourself instead.

# Salvage

Everything is broken. That's what makes it useful.

**Salvage** is the act of recovering resources, relics, or memories from the decaying world. It's not always clear what you're looking for — only that *something* must be found before night falls again.

## Mechanics

- Can be performed in ruins, wreckage zones, abandoned structures, or after battles.
- Costs moderate **Fatigue** and carries a risk of injury or contamination.
- Success influenced by:
  - Gear (e.g., gloves, crowbar, filter mask)
  - Environmental conditions (fog, wind, fungal presence)
  - Player's current **Clarity** and **Morale**

## Salvage Targets

- **Parts** – Mechanical scrap, wiring, broken tech
- **Materials** – Herbs, fungal compounds, textiles, chemicals
- **Loot** – Data cores, manuals, maps, upgrade kits, sealed meds
- **Fragments** – Myth-objects, memory remnants, broken whispers

## Risk Outcomes

- **Injury** – Cuts, punctures, infection (damages Health)
- **Despair** – Disturbing discoveries may lower Morale or trigger Gravity shift
- **False Finds** – Non-functional tech or cursed items (auto-burn on next rest)

Some regions may yield only poisoned soil and dust. Others... remember who last touched their bones.

> What you find says less about the world — and more about what you were hoping to find.

# Eat

This isn't about hunger. This is about *continuing*.

**Eat** is a simple but vital act. It replenishes **Hunger**, slows **Fatigue**, and stabilizes **Clarity** or **Morale** depending on the food consumed.

## Food Types:

- **Basic Rations** – Dry, portable, functional.
  - Moderate Hunger recovery
  - No other effect

- **Prepared Meals** (Safe Zones only)
  - High Hunger recovery
  - Moderate Morale boost
  - May trigger positive Chronicle flashback (if regional match occurs)

- **Spoiled or Dubious Food**
  - Still usable, but:
    - May cause temporary Clarity loss
    - Chance of infection or event-based debuff
    - Eating twice in a row increases risk of hallucination

- **Symbolic Foods** – Extremely rare items with cultural or personal value
  - Eating these may:
    - Trigger a unique lore sequence
    - Alter Gravity (if eaten during high-emotion events)
    - Unlock hidden survivor dialogue

Food spoils over time unless sealed or kept in cooled containers. Some food is regional — eating it far from home may affect Gravity unexpectedly.

> "We broke bread. No words. Just chewing. And in that silence, we remembered being human."

# Analyze

The world doesn't give you answers. It gives you fragments. And it dares you to understand them.

**Analyze** is the action of interpreting found fragments in a Safe Zone. Until analyzed, fragments are inert — weightless thoughts. Once understood, they become memory, truth, or infection.

## Mechanics

- Can only be performed in Safe Zones or at rare Analyzer stations.
- Costs **Mental Clarity** — the more corrupted or complex the fragment, the greater the cost.
- Some fragments require specific tools, knowledge, or regional alignment.

## Outcomes

- Adds the fragment to the **Living Chronicle**
- May:
  - Alter **Gravity** (morality-based narrative weight)
  - Unlock new structures, vaults, or dialogue options
  - Modify regional lore, shift Safe Zone behavior
  - Trigger rare Echo Traces or survivor reactions

## Fail Conditions

- Low Clarity may cause:
  - Misinterpretation (wrong entry logged)
  - False memory (fragment mutates into myth)
  - Burnout (temporarily disables further analysis)

You may only carry so much unspoken truth before it starts to *speak back*.

> "It wasn't just a fragment. It was *what I did with it.*"

# Craft

Hope isn't found. It's built — from rust, from rot, from whatever's left.

**Craft** is how players create, repair, or upgrade gear using **Parts**, **Materials**, and sometimes **Loot**. It's the main method of improving survival odds and unlocking mechanical options.

## Mechanics

- Available at:
  - **Field Crafting** (anywhere, limited)
  - **Workbench** (Safe Zones only, full access)
- Requires appropriate schematics, materials, and sometimes tools or relics
- Time-consuming — may leave the player exposed unless in a secure area

## Crafting Possibilities

- **Gear Repairs** – Fix filter masks, vests, holsters
- **Trap Creation** – Frag, tripwire, fungal lures
- **Consumables** – Tonics, stim salves, gas neutralizers
- **Upgrades** – Sidearm capacity, stealth mufflers, reactive boots
- **Decode** – Translate Loot (e.g., data cores, encrypted fragments)

## Failure States

- Low Clarity or missing tools can result in:
  - Resource waste
  - Gear degradation
  - Silent failures (item crafted but cursed/ineffective)

Optional bonuses:
- Crafting in a region aligned with your origin may boost success chance.
- Survivors nearby may offer assistance if trust is earned.

> "I made a weapon out of a rusted pipe and my grandfather's lie. It worked."

# Interact

You don't touch the world in Fractured World. You *confront* it.

**Interact** is the umbrella action that governs all contextual, reactive behavior. It is not always available — sometimes, silence is the only option.

## Use Cases

- **People** – Speak, trade, intimidate, persuade, or remain silent
- **Objects** – Open, examine, burn, carry, disable, destroy
- **Structures** – Attempt entry, trigger mechanisms, dismantle or reroute
- **Creatures** – (Rare) may include observing, soothing, or distracting

## Influencing Factors

- Current **Gravity**, **Clarity**, and **Morale**
- Regional history and survivor beliefs
- Player choices logged in the **Living Chronicle**
- Equipped gear, visible relics, or known fragments

Some objects only respond if you're *ready*. Some people only speak if you've forgotten what you once knew.

> "I didn't say a word. And that's why she told me everything."

# Safe Zone Types

Safety is a *temporary arrangement*.

Not all Safe Zones are built alike. Each one has its own traits, functions, and decay.

## Core Types

- **Sanctum** – Fully functional base with:
  - Full rest and craft capability
  - Safe fragment analysis
  - Vault access and long-term storage
  - NPC presence (if narrative allows)

- **Refuge** – Partial shelter, temporary or mobile
  - Allows rest, field repairs, and partial fragment analysis
  - No upgrades or deep crafting

- **Echo Shelter** – Tied to memory or Gravity thresholds
  - May appear only after key events
  - Offers unique dialogue, one-time Chronicle entries, or rare dreams

- **Dead Zones (Corrupted)** – Former Safe Zones overtaken by Hum, spores, or betrayal
  - Dangerous. May still allow sleep, but with high chance of negative effects.

> Safe Zones do not protect you from the world. They protect the *world* from remembering you.

# World Navigation

You don't travel in a straight line. You drift — guided by fog, memory, instinct, and need.

**World Navigation** in Fractured World is not a top-down map. It is node-based, emotionally reactive, and partially obfuscated.

## Navigation Features

- **Path Nodes** – Players move between known or discovered locations
- **Travel Cost** – Movement drains **Fatigue**, increases **Anomaly Risk** (if later added)
- **Unstable Routes** – Some paths only open/close during Hum shifts, Cycle alignments, or after specific fragment analysis
- **False Paths** – Occasionally appear if Clarity is low or Gravity is highly negative

## Compass & Region Impact

- Players from The Americas start with a damaged compass — slightly improves fog travel
- Each region may have unique navigation benefits (e.g., map fragments, instinct traits, ritual stones)

> "The map didn't change. *You* did. That's why you can't go back."

# Companion Mechanics

They are not allies. They are mirrors. And sometimes, they are dead weight.

Companions in Fractured World are rare, temporary, and not always reliable. They bring lore, utility, and narrative depth — but they also bring risk.

## Key Traits

- **Trust Level** – Grows through shared meals, protection, and dialogue
- **Burden** – Most companions slow movement or require care (e.g., meds, food)
- **Fragment Memory** – Some react to fragments the player has analyzed
- **Unique Abilities** – One-time or conditional boosts (e.g., auto-revive, stealth entry, dream translation)

## Departure

Companions do not stay forever. They may:
- Leave after a milestone (e.g., reaching home)
- Betray the player (if Gravity or Chronicle history supports it)
- Die — sometimes quietly, sometimes dramatically

Optional: Player may log a final **Companion Fragment** upon separation or death.

> "He didn't say goodbye. Just left his boots. That was enough."

# Hazards

The world ended loudly. What remains is quieter — and more cruel.

Hazards in Fractured World are not just environmental — they are psychological, fungal, and sometimes *impossible*. They are not always announced. Some are only recognized after they've already changed you.

## 🔥 Collapse Zones

- Areas prone to structural instability (e.g., ruined cities, scorched tunnels)
- Triggers:
  - Movement through unstable ground
  - Failed Salvage or Craft attempts
- Effects:
  - Instant Health loss or forced relocation
  - May separate player from companion
  - Can trap loot or fragment traces beneath rubble

> "The silence afterward was louder than the fall."

## 🌫️ Fungal Spores (Active Bloom)

- Ambient biohazards tied to Gravebloomer spread
- Triggers:
  - Entering contaminated zones without filtration gear
- Effects:
  - Clarity and Fatigue rapidly decline
  - Vision distortion or hallucinations
  - Spore-based Echo Traces may trigger involuntarily
  - Can cause mutation if exposed too long (future mechanic potential)

> "I didn't breathe it in. It breathed me *out.*"

## 🧠 Psychic Feedback (Hum Spike)

- Sudden, unexplained burst of Hum resonance
- Triggers:
  - Analyzing specific fragments
  - Safe Zone destabilization
  - Deep Gravity changes (positive or negative extremes)
- Effects:
  - Randomized companion dialogue shifts
  - Survivor betrayal chance increases
  - Temporary loss of map clarity or misremembered locations
  - Rarely spawns a false fragment (non-analyzable)

> "The sound came from nowhere. But the memory was real."

## 🧍‍♂️ Parasitic Mimics

- Infected individuals imitating survivor behavior
- Triggers:
  - Entering crowded ruins, dense buildings, or abandoned Safe Zones
  - Accepting help from unknown survivors
- Effects:
  - Delayed attacks (may follow player for hours before revealing)
  - Steal fragments or reduce Morale before combat even begins
  - False dialogue cues (speak in quotes you've heard before)

> "She used my brother's voice. But he died last Cycle."

## ⛔ Memory Failsafe Traps

- Left behind by survivors who feared the infection *inside their minds*
- Triggers:
  - Analyzing relics in cursed zones
  - Opening secured vaults or data cores
- Effects:
  - Trigger a false Living Chronicle entry (until corrected)
  - Replace a known location with a myth variant
  - Fatigue spike and temporary inability to rest

> "I remembered something that never happened. And it felt better than truth."

# Weather Hazards

In Fractured World, the weather is not background flavor — it is another enemy. It distorts travel, memory, and sometimes the very shape of the map.

Weather effects are not cosmetic. They alter perception, fragment discovery, and player performance in subtle or devastating ways.

## 🌫 Fogwall Drift

- Dense, creeping fog that shifts region paths and suppresses sound.
- Triggers:
  - Region instability
  - After a high-Gravity event
- Effects:
  - Travel paths reroll or vanish temporarily
  - Echo Traces become muffled or inaccessible
  - Lowers Clarity passively until shelter is found

> "I left with five bullets. I returned with four. And no memory of the fifth."

## ⚡ Electrical Storm

- Atmospheric discharge storms from collapsed sky-grid systems or surviving weather towers.
- Triggers:
  - Unstable areas near Old World ruins
- Effects:
  - Disables all electronics (compasses, jammers, analyzers)
  - Prevents crafting of any tech-based items
  - May randomly destroy one active gear item (roll based)

> "Lightning struck the old tower. And it screamed."

## 🧊 Black Cold

- Sudden, absolute drops in temperature. Usually lasts one cycle.
- Triggers:
  - Mountain regions, polar zones, deep night phases
- Effects:
  - Accelerates Fatigue and Morale loss
  - Doubles Hunger rate
  - Prolonged exposure reduces Craft success and causes hallucinated fragments

> "My breath froze to my lips. I couldn't tell if the thing watching me was real."

## 🌪 Wind Shear

- Horizontal gale-force winds, often carrying debris or fungal seeds.
- Triggers:
  - Plains, coastal ruins, or after spore blooms
- Effects:
  - Disorients navigation (false map routes)
  - May disable Rest unless underground
  - Physical damage if moving through exposed terrain (minor Health loss or companion injury)

> "The wind didn't blow *through* me. It tried to take *something* with it."

<!-- /show-if-event-occurred --># The Old World

When the world fractured, so too did its memory. Maps turned to myth. Names to echoes. Yet some structures from before the Fall remain—scarred, buried, repurposed. They are bones of the Old World, humming faintly with purpose no longer understood. Some call them relics. Others, omens. In their shadow, the present scrapes by.

## Old World Structures

These remnants are not ruins in the traditional sense. They were designed to outlast wars, pandemics, and perhaps even judgment itself. They do not sleep—they wait. Their lights may be dark, their doors rusted shut, but some still breathe with power.

### Core Sites

The Core Sites were once part of a vast emergency infrastructure built in the years before the Bloom—when collapse was still just a theory in dusty think-tanks and whispered war rooms. Believed to be hardened bunkers, data centers, research vaults, or command nexuses, each Core Site bore a designation code and a singular directive: survive what others could not.

Most Core Sites are buried beneath earth, ice, or ruin. Some still hum. Others scream.

#### Core Networks

The Core Sites were connected—once. Through encrypted relays, fiber-optic webs, and satellite uplinks. Survivors speak of **Core Networks** as both lifeline and trap. Some explorers have found caches of old transmissions still repeating: cryptic logs, distress calls, or looping protocols that respond to nothing and no one.

A few claim the Networks are awakening, like dormant neurons remembering their purpose. Others fear they are bait—designed to draw scavengers into forgotten teeth.

#### Core Shaft Theta

The most notorious of the Core Sites, **Core Shaft Theta** appears in multiple fragments, journals, and testimonies scattered across **The Living Chronicles**. Located somewhere beneath the wastelands of the Old World, Shaft Theta is said to descend kilometers into the rock, where experimental efforts to halt the Necromyce bloom were conducted.

Rumors speak of gene forges, fungal vivisections, and technologies lost to both ethics and time. No one who enters Shaft Theta returns unscarred—if they return at all.

But those who do return whisper that something still lives down there. Or waits.

#### Bastions

Before the bloom, when governments still pretended control, **Bastions** were fortress-cities meant to shield the remnants of civilization. Armed, self-contained, and theoretically unbreachable, these strongholds became symbols of false hope.

Some survived for years. Some fell in days.

Today, "bastion" is more a curse than a promise. Survivors use the word bitterly—to describe anywhere people huddle behind walls and pretend the spores will not seep through. A few old bastions still stand, sealed tight, their lights flickering behind reinforced glass. No one knows what's inside anymore. Refuge... or something worse.

## Technologies

Before the fall, humankind was on the edge of technological singularity—or its own extinction. Most believe it found both.

The survivors know fragments: blinking terminals, humming coils, shattered lenses, and rusted bots with flickers of ancient purpose. The Uninfected call these Old World Technologies, though no two colonies agree on what they actually were meant to do.

Some fear them. Others worship them. A few still try to wake them.

### Apex Rigs

Exosuits built not for war, but for survival. Mining, search and rescue, even urban collapse containment. Apex Rigs were prototypes—ultra-durable and self-repairing. One Rig, called "Red Echo," is still rumored to patrol the ruins of Vancouver on its last programmed directive: "Protect the weak." Whether it's still doing that—or redefining who the weak are—is a matter of debate.

### Skycells

Orbital data vaults, now fallen. These crystalline satellites stored the entire knowledge of multiple nations, designed to crash safely during an extinction-level event. Only a handful are said to have landed intact. The data inside? Corrupted, partial, encrypted... or perhaps too dangerous to read.

A player who finds one must choose: unlock what was meant to remain sealed, or bury it again.

### Cryospheres

Experimental stasis chambers once built to preserve the elite. Massive underground chambers buried in mountains or ice shelves. The cryospheres were supposed to awaken after the collapse ended.

None have opened. Yet some show signs of life inside—movement, shifting power signatures, and distorted pulses mimicking The Hum. Not all that wakes is still human.

## Lost Projects

Governments and corporations saw the Fall coming. Their last efforts were grand, rushed, and in many cases, catastrophic.

These were the Lost Projects—massive undertakings designed to outlive the infection, or escape it.

### Project Tholos

A subterranean arcology built beneath the old Mediterranean basin. Designed to house 10,000, preserve seed banks, genetic records, and culture itself. According to **The Living Chronicles**, all transmissions ceased 3 weeks after it went dark. Now, it exists only as a myth among Uninfected scholars—a whisper that echoes like a promise.

### The Elevator

A skyhook tether—meant to ferry survivors into orbit. It failed before it launched. What remains is a carbon-fiber strand thousands of kilometers long, coiled like a fallen god across half a continent. Some say entire Gravebloomer colonies live within its hollow fibers.

### Silosong Protocol

An AI-driven archive of global art, music, language, and memory—scattered to hundreds of droneborne vaults across the globe. A final act of cultural preservation. Most vaults are long lost, but a few "sing" to nearby receivers—low-frequency pings that, when decoded, play fragments of old songs, poetry, or lullabies.

Some believe The Gravebloomers hunt them for this reason. That something in those frequencies touches even what they've become.

## Extinct Organizations

When the Necromyce Bloom swept across the Earth, empires fell faster than their flags could be lowered. But not all perished quietly. Some burned brightly in their final moments—buying time, seeking a cure, or trying to preserve power.

Now, their names echo in ruins, encrypted caches, rusted insignia, and whispered curses. They were governments, corporations, resistance movements—each a titan in their own right. And each, inevitably, fell.

### The Dominion Initiative

Founded as a pan-global emergency coalition, the **Dominion Initiative** sought to quarantine the infection and establish authoritarian control to "preserve order through unity." They built **Core Sites**, **Bastions**, and control towers across major population centers.

At first, they seemed effective—until their broadcasts grew erratic, their orders contradictory. Some believe the leadership was overtaken by **Gravebloomers**. Others say internal power struggles tore it apart. One truth remains: many of their locked facilities still hold supplies, weapons, or data. But few dare try the biometric locks. Dominion tech doesn't forget.

### The Haven Network

A civilian resistance movement that formed online before the Collapse severed global comms. **The Haven Network** was a patchwork of digital sanctuaries—encrypted channels, hidden radio towers, drone drops for survivors. Their agents were known only by bird calls in the night or coded glyphs left on walls.

They vanished with little warning, but their caches remain. It's said some still pulse faintly with power, waiting for the right access phrase. And the right kind of hope.

### Aevum Biotek

One of the world's foremost biotech conglomerates, **Aevum Biotek** was rumored to have dabbled in gene-splicing, mycelial augmentation, and neurofungal interfaces—before the Necromyce ever bloomed.

Their headquarters was obliterated during the Fifth Bloom War. But pieces survived: test pods in the arctic, vaults beneath desert labs, data fragments encoded in fungal growth. Some say Aevum created Necromyce. Others think they tried to stop it—and failed.

### The Crimson Cross

A breakaway paramilitary offshoot of a global medical NGO. **The Crimson Cross** was formed to carry out triage under impossible conditions. At first, they were medics. Then they became executioners—eliminating infected zones with precision and fire.

Survivors remember the white masks. The branded chests. The sound of sterilizer drones before fire dropped from the sky. Their creed was: _"Cleanse, Contain, Continue."_ Their legacy? Fear... and often, a working medical kit if you're lucky enough to find their gear.

### The Vaulted Eye

A secretive think-tank turned intelligence syndicate. **The Vaulted Eye** predicted the bloom, documented the early mutations, and warned governments to act. Nobody listened. After the fall, they turned inward—fleeing to deep bunkers, harvesting information like survival rations.

No one has seen a Vaulted Eye agent in decades. But messages sometimes appear in forgotten terminals: offers, trades, strange requests. Perhaps they still exist. Perhaps they are watching. Or perhaps the Eye finally blinked—and what stared back drove it mad.

## Post-Collapse Belief Systems

> "Faith does not die—it mutates."

### The Bloomward Faith

Not every survivor fears the Bloom. The Bloomward Faith teaches that Necromyce is not a curse, but an evolution. Followers willingly expose themselves to spores, hoping to become Graced Ones—half-infected beings who claim communion with the fungus. Most die screaming. A rare few survive... and change.

These zealots are known for intricate fungal tattoos, spore-carved scripture, and the ritual planting of the dead. They leave bloom totems near survivor camps as "invitations."

Some are rumoured to have joined their local Gravebloomer colonies. None of those were ever heard from again.

### The Salted Creed

Where spores cling to damp earth, **The Salted Creed** believes dryness is salvation. Their enclaves exist only in salt flats, deserts, and scorched ruins. They drink little, speak rarely, and salt everything—from food to the thresholds of their tents.

Their rituals are stark and quiet. A cough in their ranks is cause for exile or burial. They carry pouches of salt like talismans and believe the fungal curse was born from human excess and indulgence, with the exception of salt, of course.

### The Hollow Choir

Born from madness, **The Hollow Choir** worships _The Hum_ itself. Not the creatures. The sound. They mimic it in rituals, chant in dissonant tones, and scar their vocal cords to better resonate with the frequencies of Blightspore dialects.

They believe _The Hum_ is a divine frequency—the voice of the Necromyce mind. Some say they walk among Blightspores unharmed, others that their rites summon horrors best left undisturbed. Their most devout disappear into infected zones and are never seen again.

### The Crimson Sheaf

A brutal offshoot of Gravebloomer ideology, **The Crimson Sheaf** reveres those who choose infection. They believe Gravebloomers were "called" and that to refuse the call is to deny the new divine order.

Their members—humans, not yet infected—don the skins of the dead and carve mycelial patterns into their flesh. They seek to be chosen, to be infected not by chance, but by worth. Gravebloomer Bishops often tolerate them... until they ask too much.

### The Last Shepherds

A dying faith, but a defiant one. **The Last Shepherds** cling to the memory of the old world—not its technologies, but its morals, its beauty. They carry battered books, teach children to write, and sing songs in forgotten languages.

They believe the Uninfected have a duty: not merely to survive, but to preserve what once was. Most are hunted by cults, raiders, or worse. But rumors speak of a "Silent Library" far in the wastes, where they protect pre-Bloom knowledge at all costs.

## Starting Region Bonuses and Trade Mechanic

Depending on your chosen starting region, you begin the game with a minor survival advantage — either an inherited item, local knowledge, or environmental resistance. These bonuses are subtle, thematic, and vary slightly in gameplay effect.

Each region provides:

- A **starting item**, with light practical use.

### The Trade Option

At the beginning of your journey, you'll be presented with a choice:

- **Keep** your starting item for its practical benefit.
- **Trade** it at the first **merchant**, **forsaken cache**, or **salvage vault** you encounter. The game will generate **2 randomized barter offers**, based on your region's inventory.

💡 You may **still trade the item** later with NPCs, **up to the first time you attempt to use it**. Once used, it becomes bound to your inventory and cannot be traded.

### Structures and Systems

The old world left behind more than ruin. Across the wastes, survivors whisper of rare entities and locations that still offer a glimpse of what once was — or what could be again. These structures are few, but their influence is immense.

In the fractured remains of the old world, the title of "merchant" no longer means prosperity. It means movement, cunning, and just enough trust to survive one more deal. These individuals travel the deadlands with patched carts, mutated pack beasts, or solar-powered bikes cobbled from rusted dreams. They trade in parts, rumors, and memories. Some are former engineers. Some are killers. A few are said to be former Gravebloomers who found a way to coexist with the infection. All of them carry relics of the old world — often more useful than you'd expect.

> "You got food, I got filters. You got stories, I got bullets. Let's pretend the world still spins, yeah?"

- Merchants can appear in ruined outposts, crossroads, or safe zones.
- Inventory rotates based on region, time passed, and major world events.
- Not all merchants are honest. Some trade hope for poison.

#### Forsaken Caches

There are places in the world where someone once tried to survive — and failed. Tucked behind false walls, beneath floorboards, or buried under scorched soil, these caches tell forgotten stories. Family bunkers, lone survivor stashes, even military drops lost in chaos. To the desperate, a forsaken cache is salvation. Or a grave.

> "No footsteps. No Hum. Just dust and a prayer."

- Require sharp eyes or old-world maps to locate.
- May contain one-time-use barter options — rare materials or obscure relics.
- Caches can be trapped, decayed, or guarded by opportunistic scavengers.

#### Salvage Vaults

Before the collapse, salvage vaults were government fallback sites, corporate black vaults, or sealed prepper tombs meant to outlast any disaster. Some still remain locked — sealed by biometric failsafes, radiation fields, or fungal overgrowth. Others have cracked open, spilling secrets into the dust.

> "We built bunkers to preserve the future. We forgot we had to survive to open them."

- High-stakes loot zones. Extremely rare and heavily guarded or condition-gated.
- May offer **powerful trades**, story-altering relics, or deep lore fragments.
- Access might require multiple steps: keycards, override codes, or Gravebloomer relics.
