# Asia and Surrounds

**Asia**, the most populous continent, fractured spectacularly under the weight of the Necromyce catastrophe. In the East, ancient cities drowned under fungal blooms and desperate migrations. In the South, chaotic megacities collapsed into feral ruins, while in the North, remnants of order cling to survival in fortified enclaves. Across this vast and broken land, the old boundaries of nation and creed have blurred, replaced by brutal pragmatism—and an ever-present fear of what lurks beyond the crumbling skyline.

Beneath the mountains' silent watch, monks planted ironwood trees and whispered prayers to the old gods. Something answered.

## Natural Disasters

Earthquakes, typhoons, floods and tsunamis.

## Minerals and Resources

Rare earth metals, coal and forests.

## Survival Insight

Some villages and rural strongholds resisted longer due to isolation and centuries of spiritual preservation techniques.

## Precision from chaos. Shadows in numbers.

Those from this vast region benefit from generational exposure to crowded survival. Minor bonuses in stealth within dense urban ruins, and a slight edge in negotiating or navigating old transport systems like subways and skyways.

## Starting Inventory

🎭 Gravebloomer Disguise Mask - Replica used in urban cult resistance. Wearing it reduces detection by Mimics slightly. Highly valuable in trade with cult-friendly NPCs.

## Regional Landmarks and Remnants

### The Crater Monastery of Tibet

A hollowed peak where monks are said to have survived the Bloom's first wave in total silence. Recent visitors report distant chanting—and no signs of life.

### The Pearl Spine of the South China Sea

A chain of fungal reefs formed on the husks of sunken trade ships. Glows faintly in moonlight. Nomadic scavengers speak of rare tech entombed in salt and bloom.

### The Forgotten Rail of Siberia

A bullet train locked in perpetual transit, its AI system corrupted. Satellite heat signatures suggest it still moves across the tundra without passengers—or with them.

## Old World Timeline Highlights

### 2020 – Fall of the Red Lantern Network

A media blackout across mainland China during the first fungal wave cut millions off from public knowledge. When transmissions resumed, major cities were already overrun. The silence was later called """The Eight-Day Eclipse."

### 2021 – Himalayan Barrier Collapse

In a desperate effort to contain cross-border infection, several passes into the Himalayas were sealed with explosives. The resulting avalanches protected some valleys but buried entire mountain villages forever.

### 2022 – The Nameless Broadcast in India

A sequence of encrypted signals pulsed across the subcontinent. No origin was ever located. Decoders insist the pattern included Sanskrit, binary, and Gravebloomer vocal harmonics. The final message translates loosely to: *"It waits for your breath."*

## Historic Cities

Across Asia, cities were pressure cookers of life—densely populated, feverishly built, never truly still. When Necromyce arrived, that density turned deadly. The infection moved faster than word, faster than help. Today, what remains of these cities is a haunting mix of steel, vines, sacred altars, and silent sprawl. Every corner hides something old, something lost, or something watching.

### Tokyo, Japan

Once a glowing monument to technology and precision, **Tokyo** fell swiftly. In a city built vertically, the infection raced through the subways and skyscrapers alike, leaving behind towering vertical graveyards. Some of the tallest structures now sway under the weight of fungal overgrowth, while small survivor enclaves cling to the lower ruins, illuminated only by the faint remnants of neon.

### Shanghai, China

**Shanghai**'s glittering skyline faded beneath the choking breath of Necromyce faster than anyone believed possible. The Huangpu River now carries spores as easily as it once carried commerce. Though the city's upper districts are largely abandoned, rumors whisper of strange new civilizations forming deep within its cavernous underground metro networks.

### Mumbai, India

**Mumbai** was already a city of extreme contrasts—wealth and poverty colliding block by block. When the infection struck, it exploited every crack in the city's defenses. Crowded slums became death traps; opulent towers became tombs. Yet amidst the chaos, Mumbai's fierce spirit persists. Nomadic bands, many formed from former film stars and artists, now roam the ruins, preserving stories and art as sacred relics of a lost world.

### Seoul, South Korea

**Seoul** resisted longer than most, fortified by technology, surveillance, and brutal lockdowns. But no wall, no drone, no quarantine could halt the inevitable. Now, Seoul's sprawling urban maze teems with Blightspore nests, while the few surviving humans have taken to living underground, emerging only under cover of night, fearful of both the infected—and each other.

### Jakarta, Indonesia

**Jakarta**'s tropical humidity became a death sentence when the Necromyce spores arrived. The city drowned under layers of moss, vines, and fungal rot, turning its towering skyline into ghostly green monoliths. Tribes of Gravebloomers now rule the upper levels of long-abandoned skyscrapers, battling each other and whatever horrors still crawl in the steamy depths below.
