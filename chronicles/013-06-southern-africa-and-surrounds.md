# Southern Africa and Surrounds

**Southern Africa**, once a land of breathtaking contrasts—from sun-scorched deserts to lush savannahs and coastal paradises—became a battlefield where nature itself turned against its people. In some remote areas, communities resisted the Necromyce Bloom longer than most, shielded by distance and resilience. But no refuge lasted forever. Today, remnants of humanity cling stubbornly to the bones of once-great cities, forging fragile new societies beneath an endless, infected sky.

Gold still glitters beneath Johannesburg—but these days, it's teeth, not coins, that guard it.

## Natural Disasters

Droughts, flash floods and locust swarms.

## Minerals and Resources

Gold, diamonds, uranium and fertile plateaus.

## Survival Insight

Desert clans may control trade in rare isotopes; former mining shafts now house colonies or entomb horrors.

## Sun-hardened. Deep-ground thinkers.

Born from lands where shelter meant digging, not building—players here have a better grasp of subterranean navigation and structural stability. Slight bonus when creating underground camps or detecting environmental hazards before collapse.

## Starting Inventory

🛠️ Mining Scanner Fragment - Old tech from a survey drone. Can locate metal-rich ruins or hidden stashes within short range. Can be traded for rare ammo (4 bullets) or a scavenger's relic blade.

## Regional Landmarks and Remnants

### The Barren Mouth at the Namibia–Botswana border

A dried salt pan that emits a low hum at dusk. Surveyors report seeing sinkholes open and close silently beneath the cracked earth. Believed to house at least one dormant Titan-class Blightspore.

### The Diamond Spine of South Africa

An abandoned series of subterranean tunnels once used for deep-shaft diamond mining. Now partially collapsed and spore-infested. Some say survivors still live inside—others say they never left.

### The Rain Chain of Mozambique

A vertical cliff face riddled with hanging gardens, fungal colonies, and forgotten bunkers. During rare storms, bioluminescent sporecaps bloom across its face, attracting both scavengers and predators.

## Old World Timeline Highlights

### 2020 – Shutdown of the Garden Route

Tourist towns from George to Knysna were evacuated in waves when unexplained fungal sightings were confirmed along forest trails. Military quarantine was brief—and ineffective.

### 2021 – Johannesburg Collapse

A mass transit system shutdown triggered a full-scale urban panic. Emergency shelters were overrun, and the central business district was consumed within 48 hours. Evacuation corridors became grave routes.

### 2022 – The Windhoek Accord

A regional summit of surviving southern nations was held in Windhoek. Lasted two days. All participating envoys vanished en route home. No records exist of what was agreed upon, but one surviving envoy wrote: *"It wasn't politics that made us quiet—it was the sound beneath the floor."*

## Historic Cities

Southern Africa's cities stood like sentinels—half forged in steel, half grown from earth. They had endured colonization, revolution, and neglect, but the Necromyce offered no foe to fight. Instead, it seeped in quietly: through the ports, through the dust, through the desperate. Now, the region's cities are wild mosaics—some choked by vines, some buried in sand, and some teetering between salvation and oblivion.

## Cape Town, South Africa

Nestled between mountains and sea, **Cape Town** fought valiantly to shield its people behind hastily constructed quarantine walls. But the infection seeped in through the ports, the farmlands, and the refugee camps. Now, the city is a fractured jewel: crumbling colonial districts swallowed by spores, while isolated colonies cling to the slopes of Table Mountain, wary both of the Blightspores below and each other.

## Johannesburg, South Africa

Once a powerhouse of commerce and culture, **Johannesburg**'s gleaming skyscrapers became tombstones almost overnight. Mass evacuations turned highways into graveyards of rusted cars and desperate refugees. Today, "Jozi"' is a dangerous sprawl of broken buildings and contested territories, where Gravebloomer clans and uninfected warily navigate a world where gold no longer holds any value—but food, water, and loyalty do.

## Windhoek, Namibia

The arid expanses around **Windhoek** delayed the spread of infection, giving rise to stubborn pockets of survivors who adapted quickly to the new world. Makeshift fortresses dot the desert sands, while scavengers risk the deadly heat to trade salvaged technology. Windhoek remains a rare prize: a functioning hub of survival—if you can survive the treacherous journey to reach it.

## Antananarivo, Madagascar

Isolation bought Madagascar time, but not salvation. The capital, **Antananarivo**, was consumed slowly and painfully. Jungle vines now entwine the colonial architecture, while fungal blooms choke the old marketplaces. Strange new sects have risen among the survivors here, blending old island spirituality with grim devotion to surviving the Necromyce, no matter the cost.

## Kinshasa, Democratic Republic of the Congo

Sprawling along the banks of the Congo River, **Kinshasa** was once a vibrant, chaotic heart of Central Africa. But its vitality became its downfall when Necromyce found its way through its arteries. Bridges collapsed, districts burned, and the river itself became a perilous barrier between the desperate living and the encroaching infected. Today, the ruins of Kinshasa are whispered about with awe and dread: a place where ancient spirits, blooming graveyards, and stubborn colonies cling to the battered edges of survival.
