# ☍ Gravity Mechanic

## Gravity

Gravity is a hidden value adjusted throughout gameplay based on:

- Actions taken (e.g., mercy vs. ruthlessness)
- Fragments uncovered (e.g., hopeful lore vs. nihilistic revelations)
- Events experienced (e.g., survivor betrayals, discovered atrocities)

At the end, a "voice" appears — not a real person, but a manifestation of accumulated moral weight.

## Depending on the Gravity score

- Significantly below 0 -> the voice tempts, condemns, seduces: "End it. Let the bloom finish what it began."
- Significantly above 0 -> the voice encourages, questions, inspires: "“"There is still light. A future waits in blood and ash."
- Neutral (insignificantly above or below 0, nobody knows the exact threshold) -> the voice is uncertain: "“"Nothing is certain. Nothing is promised. But you still choose."

# Fragment Analysis

Fragments are found, not understood. Until they are brought to safety, they remain dormant — no more than riddles in glass or echoes etched in metal.

To analyze a fragment, the player must reach a Safe Zone and initiate the **Fragment Analysis** ritual. This may take time or resources, depending on the zone’s type. Once analyzed:

- If the fragment is **mundane or broken**, it may add flavor or world-building detail, but not affect gameplay.
- If the fragment is **myth-bound**, it may shift the tone or content of the Living Chronicle, marking the event as myth or memory.
- If the fragment is **high-value** (often rare or region-specific), it may:
  - Alter player morality (Gravity).
  - Unlock new areas or survivors.
  - Trigger passive memory shifts, unlocking backlogged lore or spoken echoes.
  - Set up delayed or chained events.

Unanalyzed fragments may still carry faint atmospheric weight — slight warmth, hallucinations, or Hum interference — but they cannot shape the world until read under safety and silence.

Fragments are not inventory. They are *beliefs waiting for proof.*

# Safe Zones

Safe Zones are the only places where time stabilizes and the player can act without immediate consequence. There is no universal layout — some are crumbling shelters, others bio-sealed bunkers, even ancient stone circles humming with stillness.

Inside a Safe Zone:

- Players may **analyze fragments**, **rest**, **eat**, **repair**, or **commune** with echo traces.
- Certain zones allow **rituals**, **resonance tuning**, or access to long-forgotten **vaults**.
- Safe Zones also **reset Anomaly Pressure** and temporarily suppress Hum effects.
- If present, other survivors may offer trades, stories, or regional rumors (often linked to the player’s Living Chronicle state).

Safe Zones are not *safe* in the traditional sense. They are stable — for now. Each one has a **Signal Stability Rating**, which decays over time or with overuse. Once unstable, its functions begin to falter.

Some Safe Zones move. Others appear only during certain Cycles.

Every survivor remembers their first Safe Zone. Fewer remember their last.

# Starting Regions

The place you woke defines more than just geography — it whispers through every choice you make. Starting Regions in *Fractured World* determine:

- **Opening Inventory** — a tool, relic, or map echoing that region’s final days.
- **Passive Trait** — slight buffs unique to each location (e.g., resistance to mountain fatigue, stealth in fungal mist, crafting precision).
- **Subtle Dialogue Variants** — some survivors react differently based on your accent, scent, or inherited silence.

Each region has three known Trade Opportunities:
1. **Forsaken Cache** — bury your bonus, and walk away. The story might find you later.
2. **Salvage Vault** — exchange for items or fragments with mechanical value.
3. **Nomad Merchant** — barter for unpredictable, lore-rich gear.

These trades are **permanent**, and only one can be claimed. Once the choice is made, the others fade.

The region you started in will be remembered — by you, by the world, and by those who listen for your voice in the Hum.

# Environmental Gauges

You are more than just alive. You are breaking, surviving, forgetting, enduring — all at once. The world does not care about numbers. But *you* must.

The player is tracked by several environmental gauges. They are not always visible — only when relevant, fractured, or at the brink of collapse.

## Core Gauges:

- **Health** – Physical condition. Reduced by wounds, infection, or extreme cold. Restored by rest, food, or rare meds.
- **Hunger** – Starvation increases Fatigue and lowers Clarity. Prolonged hunger also causes hallucinations during fog cycles.
- **Fatigue** – Influences action success. High fatigue causes failed crafts, poor reaction time, or narrative instability.
- **Mental Clarity** – Determines resistance to Hum, hallucinations, and false fragments. Drops in cursed zones or after betrayal.
- **Morale** – Not joy. Not hope. Just the *will* to keep moving. Boosted by story events, survivor presence, small comforts, and food rituals.

Each gauge interacts with others. Hunger causes Fatigue. Low Clarity may distort Morale. Damage to one is rarely isolated.

Most gauges regenerate slowly in Safe Zones, but only fully recover through rituals, kindness, or choices that align with your Chronicle.

Players may temporarily suppress gauge effects — through stimulants, vows, or psychological techniques — but never without cost.

You are not a hero. You are a *barometer of collapse*. Keep your dials in the green... or redefine what green means.

# Inventory System

What you carry defines what you survive. And what you survive... defines what you become.

The player's inventory is organized into six core categories. Each is limited — not just by space, but by *tolerance*. The world only lets you carry so much before it starts asking questions.

## Inventory Categories:

### Weapons

- Primary and secondary tools of defense or aggression. Includes melee and ranged.

### Ammunition

Stored separately from weapons. Must be loaded manually when depleted.

### Gear

Survival tools, protective items, and tactical equipment.

### Loot

Salvaged or quest-critical items. Often fragmented, encrypted, or damaged.

### Parts

Raw components used for repair, upgrades, and craftable traps or tech.

### Materials

Organic or elemental matter used in chemistry, patchwork, or healing.

## Inventory Loadout Limits

Each category has a default carry cap, which may expand through upgrades, traits, or tradeoffs:

### Weapons

- Two ranged
- One melee

### Ammunition

- Pistols and revolvers: 24
- Hunting rifles and shotguns: 16
- Sub-machine guns and Assault Rifles: 80
- Flamethrowers: 150

### Gear

- Heavier gear speeds of fatigue or increases noise
- Maximum 3 items without penalty.

### Loot

- Certain items may conflict

### Parts & Materials

- Stackable but weight adds up
- High quantities may attract certain NPCs or events

### Fragments

Fragments do not use inventory slots — they use *space in the story*.

> Some items *carry you*, whether you want them to or not.

# Echo Traces

Not all memories are yours. Some cling to the earth like frost. Others arrive uninvited, whispering names you never said.

**Echo Traces** are narrative impressions left behind in places marked by trauma, decision, or resonance. They are not visible on the map, nor consistent across playthroughs. They are *felt.*

## Key Features

- Echo Traces **spawn randomly**, up to 2 times per in-game day (24-hour cycle).
- Traces may occur:
  - Near significant structures (vaults, safe zones, ruins)
  - Following major player actions (e.g., analyzing a rare fragment, crossing Gravity thresholds)
  - During environmental events (fog shifts, Hum spikes, fragment alignments)

## When Discovered

- Players may "Listen" to the Echo, revealing:
  - **Survivor quotes** (raw, often unfinished)
  - **Flashback-style visions** (brief, lore-rich, never fully explained)
  - **Fragment echoes** (indirect lore reinforcement)

## Mechanical Effect

- Echo Traces do **not** directly alter gameplay systems.
- However, each echo subtly influences **Gravity**, especially if the player engages empathetically.
- Some may alter or amplify future **Safe Zone dialogue**, **dream sequences**, or **Living Chronicle tone**.

## Optional Responses

Players may:

- Dismiss the echo
- Record it in their Chronicle
- Share it with another survivor (if one is present)

Each choice *shapes perception* — of the world, of the past, and of who you might become.

The world is watching. And sometimes, it remembers *for* you.

# The Hum

It is not a sound. It is a presence. A pressure behind the ear. A song beneath your thoughts. Survivors call it **The Hum** — and none who hear it remain unchanged.

The Hum is an ever-present environmental force. It has no source, yet it resonates through stone, bone, and silence. It is tied to the infection, the collapse, and the fragments left behind.

## Hum Frequencies and Effects:

The Hum fluctuates in intensity and pitch. Players do not "see" it — they feel its effects.

- **Low-frequency Hum**:
  - Reduces **Morale** and suppresses survivor communication.
  - May trigger echo fragments from trauma zones.

- **Mid-frequency Hum**:
  - Interferes with **Fatigue** recovery and increases fragment distortion.
  - Causes spatial disorientation or "map ghosts" in ruined zones.

- **High-frequency Hum**:
  - Reduces **Mental Clarity** and makes fake fragments appear plausible.
  - Can trigger whispered hallucinations or false survivor memories.

## Hum Events

Random or region-triggered **Hum Events** may:

- Alter the tone or availability of survivor dialogue.
- Disrupt fragment analysis if not protected.
- Temporarily shift Gravity thresholds, forcing emotional reactions in otherwise neutral decisions.

## Damping the Hum

Survivors may protect themselves — imperfectly — through:

- **Signal-dampening helmets or filter masks**
- **Portable jammers** with limited battery life
- **Ritual actions** (humming, sketching, sleep phrasing) known only to a few

Even then, the Hum listens back.

> _"We didn’t create the Hum. We simply got quiet enough to hear it."_
