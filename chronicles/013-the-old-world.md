# The Old World

When the world fractured, so too did its memory. Maps turned to myth. Names to echoes. Yet some structures from before the Fall remain—scarred, buried, repurposed. They are bones of the Old World, humming faintly with purpose no longer understood. Some call them relics. Others, omens. In their shadow, the present scrapes by.

## Old World Structures

These remnants are not ruins in the traditional sense. They were designed to outlast wars, pandemics, and perhaps even judgment itself. They do not sleep—they wait. Their lights may be dark, their doors rusted shut, but some still breathe with power.

### Core Sites

The Core Sites were once part of a vast emergency infrastructure built in the years before the Bloom—when collapse was still just a theory in dusty think-tanks and whispered war rooms. Believed to be hardened bunkers, data centers, research vaults, or command nexuses, each Core Site bore a designation code and a singular directive: survive what others could not.

Most Core Sites are buried beneath earth, ice, or ruin. Some still hum. Others scream.

#### Core Networks

The Core Sites were connected—once. Through encrypted relays, fiber-optic webs, and satellite uplinks. Survivors speak of **Core Networks** as both lifeline and trap. Some explorers have found caches of old transmissions still repeating: cryptic logs, distress calls, or looping protocols that respond to nothing and no one.

A few claim the Networks are awakening, like dormant neurons remembering their purpose. Others fear they are bait—designed to draw scavengers into forgotten teeth.

#### Core Shaft Theta

The most notorious of the Core Sites, **Core Shaft Theta** appears in multiple fragments, journals, and testimonies scattered across **The Living Chronicles**. Located somewhere beneath the wastelands of the Old World, Shaft Theta is said to descend kilometers into the rock, where experimental efforts to halt the Necromyce bloom were conducted.

Rumors speak of gene forges, fungal vivisections, and technologies lost to both ethics and time. No one who enters Shaft Theta returns unscarred—if they return at all.

But those who do return whisper that something still lives down there. Or waits.

#### Bastions

Before the bloom, when governments still pretended control, **Bastions** were fortress-cities meant to shield the remnants of civilization. Armed, self-contained, and theoretically unbreachable, these strongholds became symbols of false hope.

Some survived for years. Some fell in days.

Today, "bastion" is more a curse than a promise. Survivors use the word bitterly—to describe anywhere people huddle behind walls and pretend the spores will not seep through. A few old bastions still stand, sealed tight, their lights flickering behind reinforced glass. No one knows what's inside anymore. Refuge... or something worse.

## Technologies

Before the fall, humankind was on the edge of technological singularity—or its own extinction. Most believe it found both.

The survivors know fragments: blinking terminals, humming coils, shattered lenses, and rusted bots with flickers of ancient purpose. The Uninfected call these Old World Technologies, though no two colonies agree on what they actually were meant to do.

Some fear them. Others worship them. A few still try to wake them.

### Apex Rigs

Exosuits built not for war, but for survival. Mining, search and rescue, even urban collapse containment. Apex Rigs were prototypes—ultra-durable and self-repairing. One Rig, called "Red Echo," is still rumored to patrol the ruins of Vancouver on its last programmed directive: "Protect the weak." Whether it's still doing that—or redefining who the weak are—is a matter of debate.

### Skycells

Orbital data vaults, now fallen. These crystalline satellites stored the entire knowledge of multiple nations, designed to crash safely during an extinction-level event. Only a handful are said to have landed intact. The data inside? Corrupted, partial, encrypted... or perhaps too dangerous to read.

A player who finds one must choose: unlock what was meant to remain sealed, or bury it again.

### Cryospheres

Experimental stasis chambers once built to preserve the elite. Massive underground chambers buried in mountains or ice shelves. The cryospheres were supposed to awaken after the collapse ended.

None have opened. Yet some show signs of life inside—movement, shifting power signatures, and distorted pulses mimicking The Hum. Not all that wakes is still human.

## Lost Projects

Governments and corporations saw the Fall coming. Their last efforts were grand, rushed, and in many cases, catastrophic.

These were the Lost Projects—massive undertakings designed to outlive the infection, or escape it.

### Project Tholos

A subterranean arcology built beneath the old Mediterranean basin. Designed to house 10,000, preserve seed banks, genetic records, and culture itself. According to **The Living Chronicles**, all transmissions ceased 3 weeks after it went dark. Now, it exists only as a myth among Uninfected scholars—a whisper that echoes like a promise.

### The Elevator

A skyhook tether—meant to ferry survivors into orbit. It failed before it launched. What remains is a carbon-fiber strand thousands of kilometers long, coiled like a fallen god across half a continent. Some say entire Gravebloomer colonies live within its hollow fibers.

### Silosong Protocol

An AI-driven archive of global art, music, language, and memory—scattered to hundreds of droneborne vaults across the globe. A final act of cultural preservation. Most vaults are long lost, but a few "sing" to nearby receivers—low-frequency pings that, when decoded, play fragments of old songs, poetry, or lullabies.

Some believe The Gravebloomers hunt them for this reason. That something in those frequencies touches even what they've become.

## Extinct Organizations

When the Necromyce Bloom swept across the Earth, empires fell faster than their flags could be lowered. But not all perished quietly. Some burned brightly in their final moments—buying time, seeking a cure, or trying to preserve power.

Now, their names echo in ruins, encrypted caches, rusted insignia, and whispered curses. They were governments, corporations, resistance movements—each a titan in their own right. And each, inevitably, fell.

### The Dominion Initiative

Founded as a pan-global emergency coalition, the **Dominion Initiative** sought to quarantine the infection and establish authoritarian control to "preserve order through unity." They built **Core Sites**, **Bastions**, and control towers across major population centers.

At first, they seemed effective—until their broadcasts grew erratic, their orders contradictory. Some believe the leadership was overtaken by **Gravebloomers**. Others say internal power struggles tore it apart. One truth remains: many of their locked facilities still hold supplies, weapons, or data. But few dare try the biometric locks. Dominion tech doesn't forget.

### The Haven Network

A civilian resistance movement that formed online before the Collapse severed global comms. **The Haven Network** was a patchwork of digital sanctuaries—encrypted channels, hidden radio towers, drone drops for survivors. Their agents were known only by bird calls in the night or coded glyphs left on walls.

They vanished with little warning, but their caches remain. It's said some still pulse faintly with power, waiting for the right access phrase. And the right kind of hope.

### Aevum Biotek

One of the world's foremost biotech conglomerates, **Aevum Biotek** was rumored to have dabbled in gene-splicing, mycelial augmentation, and neurofungal interfaces—before the Necromyce ever bloomed.

Their headquarters was obliterated during the Fifth Bloom War. But pieces survived: test pods in the arctic, vaults beneath desert labs, data fragments encoded in fungal growth. Some say Aevum created Necromyce. Others think they tried to stop it—and failed.

### The Crimson Cross

A breakaway paramilitary offshoot of a global medical NGO. **The Crimson Cross** was formed to carry out triage under impossible conditions. At first, they were medics. Then they became executioners—eliminating infected zones with precision and fire.

Survivors remember the white masks. The branded chests. The sound of sterilizer drones before fire dropped from the sky. Their creed was: _"Cleanse, Contain, Continue."_ Their legacy? Fear... and often, a working medical kit if you're lucky enough to find their gear.

### The Vaulted Eye

A secretive think-tank turned intelligence syndicate. **The Vaulted Eye** predicted the bloom, documented the early mutations, and warned governments to act. Nobody listened. After the fall, they turned inward—fleeing to deep bunkers, harvesting information like survival rations.

No one has seen a Vaulted Eye agent in decades. But messages sometimes appear in forgotten terminals: offers, trades, strange requests. Perhaps they still exist. Perhaps they are watching. Or perhaps the Eye finally blinked—and what stared back drove it mad.

## Post-Collapse Belief Systems

<blockquote>"Faith does not die—it mutates."</blockquote>

### The Bloomward Faith

Not every survivor fears the Bloom. The Bloomward Faith teaches that Necromyce is not a curse, but an evolution. Followers willingly expose themselves to spores, hoping to become Graced Ones—half-infected beings who claim communion with the fungus. Most die screaming. A rare few survive... and change.

These zealots are known for intricate fungal tattoos, spore-carved scripture, and the ritual planting of the dead. They leave bloom totems near survivor camps as "invitations."

Some are rumoured to have joined their local Gravebloomer colonies. None of those were ever heard from again.

### The Salted Creed

Where spores cling to damp earth, **The Salted Creed** believes dryness is salvation. Their enclaves exist only in salt flats, deserts, and scorched ruins. They drink little, speak rarely, and salt everything—from food to the thresholds of their tents.

Their rituals are stark and quiet. A cough in their ranks is cause for exile or burial. They carry pouches of salt like talismans and believe the fungal curse was born from human excess and indulgence, with the exception of salt, of course.

### The Hollow Choir

Born from madness, **The Hollow Choir** worships _The Hum_ itself. Not the creatures. The sound. They mimic it in rituals, chant in dissonant tones, and scar their vocal cords to better resonate with the frequencies of Blightspore dialects.

They believe _The Hum_ is a divine frequency—the voice of the Necromyce mind. Some say they walk among Blightspores unharmed, others that their rites summon horrors best left undisturbed. Their most devout disappear into infected zones and are never seen again.

### The Crimson Sheaf

A brutal offshoot of Gravebloomer ideology, **The Crimson Sheaf** reveres those who choose infection. They believe Gravebloomers were "called" and that to refuse the call is to deny the new divine order.

Their members—humans, not yet infected—don the skins of the dead and carve mycelial patterns into their flesh. They seek to be chosen, to be infected not by chance, but by worth. Gravebloomer Bishops often tolerate them... until they ask too much.

### The Last Shepherds

A dying faith, but a defiant one. **The Last Shepherds** cling to the memory of the old world—not its technologies, but its morals, its beauty. They carry battered books, teach children to write, and sing songs in forgotten languages.

They believe the Uninfected have a duty: not merely to survive, but to preserve what once was. Most are hunted by cults, raiders, or worse. But rumors speak of a "Silent Library" far in the wastes, where they protect pre-Bloom knowledge at all costs.

## Continent Status

### The Americas

Before the fall, **the Americas** stood as a patchwork of soaring ambition and growing unrest. From gleaming skyscrapers to desolate rural plains, it was a land where prosperity and hardship existed side by side. When Necromyce bloomed, densely packed cities became death traps, and the sprawling open spaces offered little refuge. From the concrete canyons of New York to the sun-scorched streets of São Paulo, hope bled out into the soil almost as fast as the infection spread.

The silver veins of the Andes still glint beneath dust and moss—useless to the world that came after, yet endlessly tempting to tech-hunters who dream of trading old metals for new miracles.

**Natural Disasters**

Prone to hurricanes, earthquakes and wildfires.

**Minerals and Resources**

Rich in oil, lithium, gold and silver, and agricultural land.

**Survival Insight**

Survivors in the mountains may have fared better, relying on the minera riches for tech scavenging or geothermal heat.

**Mountain cunning. Resourceful mindset**

Those who begin here benefit from a rugged adaptability—descended from miners, engineers, and survivalists who knew the mountains better than the cities. They start with a higher chance of locating salvageable tech or geothermal warmth, and minor resistance to environmental stress.

**Starting Inventory**

🧭 Old Military Compass - Reliable, rugged, slightly scuffed. Allows slightly more accurate directional navigation in foggy/mountain regions. Can be traded for 10 parts or a weathered handgun with no rounds.

#### New York, USA

The city that never slept... sleeps now forever under fungal veils. Once the beating heart of global finance and culture, **New York**'s towering skyline now sags under the weight of decay. Central Park has become a wild thicket, home to both desperate survivors and roving Blightspore packs. The subways—once arteries of human ambition—now echo only with the Hum.

#### Los Angeles, USA

**Los Angeles** was a glittering oasis of dreams, where movies and myths were born under neon skies. When Necromyce arrived, the city's sprawling highways became deathtraps, and its opulence offered no shield. Today, the Hollywood sign lies half-swallowed by vines, and the luxury mansions of the hills stand as silent tombs.

#### Seattle, USA

**Seattle**'s endless rain once gave it a reputation for melancholy, but it was water that bought it time when the infection spread. For months, communities held out in isolation, fortified among the misty evergreens. Now, the Space Needle juts like a broken finger through grey clouds, and the forests that once protected Seattle harbor worse things than wolves.

#### São Paulo, Brazil

One of the largest cities in the southern hemisphere, **São Paulo** thrived on chaos long before the world crumbled. Skyscrapers rose like jagged teeth against the horizon, housing millions. But when Necromyce swept through, density became death. Now, the favelas sprawl endlessly under the shadow of ruined towers, and forgotten fires still flicker at night, far out in the mist.

### Eastern Europe and Surrounds

Long a crossroads of empires and battles, **Eastern Europe** bore the scars of old wars and the dreams of new beginnings. Its ancient cities, cobblestone alleys, and fortified citadels provided some of the fiercest early resistance against the Necromyce. Yet pride could not hold back decay. Governments fractured, alliances shattered, and the streets that once sang with revolution fell silent beneath fungal vines.

The Carpathians breathe steam again. Not from dragons—but reactors, buried and still hot under the frost.

**Natural Disasters**

Earthquakes, floods and extreme winters.

**Minerals and Resources**

Coal, uranium, rare forests and black soil for agriculture.

**Survival Insight**

Underground bunkers in some zones may have been reactivated and others' caves became shelters.

**Stubborn lineage. Resistance blood.**

Starting in Eastern Europe grants increased resilience to cold and a cultural instinct for hidden paths—bunkers, tunnels, ruins long forgotten. You begin with minor morale protection during isolation or loss, and a faint edge in spotting ruins from older wars.

**Starting Inventory**

🪓 Hand-Forged Hatchet - Passed down since before the Collapse. Excellent for crafting shelters and melee defense. Can be traded for 15 materials or an encrypted radio shard (useless until decoded).

#### Warsaw, Poland

**Warsaw** stood proud, rebuilt from the ashes of past wars. Its people, too stubborn to break, fought the infection street by street. For a while, it worked. Resistance bands roamed the Old Town's alleys like phantoms. But even Warsaw's spirit could not halt the inevitable. Today, fragments of patriot songs drift down deserted boulevards lined with crumbling monuments.

#### Bucharest, Romania

**Bucharest**, a city of contradictions—grand palaces beside brutalist towers—Bucharest was a labyrinth of history. When the Necromyce bloom unfurled across its streets, survival twisted into something colder. Gangs now rule the abandoned districts, trading scraps of ancient technology for water and medicine. Somewhere beneath the crumbling Parliament Palace, legends say a Gravebloomer enclave stirs.

#### Prague, Czech Republic

The City of a Hundred Spires gleamed once with romance, art, and gothic grandeur. Its stone bridges and castle walls withstood centuries of storms and sieges. But not this one. **Prague** today is a city of shadows: ivy-cloaked cathedrals, silent squares, and statues weeping fungal tears as the Vltava River quietly reclaims its banks.

#### Budapest, Hungary

Twin cities once divided by the Danube, **Budapest** was a jewel of Europe, a bridge between past and future. When collapse came, it came fast. The underground thermal baths—once a source of healing—became fetid breeding grounds for spores. Survivors whisper of strange things rising with the steam, cloaked in rot and sorrow.

#### Moscow, Russia

The iron heart of Russia, **Moscow**'s great monuments—the Kremlin, Red Square—are now crumbling bastions draped in fungal growth. Beneath the ice and rot, hidden bunkers shelter those few who refuse to abandon the motherland. The Blightspore presence here is relentless; their Hum reverberates through the underground metro tunnels, making each expedition above or below ground a gamble. Yet still, in the heart of winter, the spirit of resistance lingers like frost on broken windows.

#### St. Petersburg, Russia

Once called Russia's window to the West, **St. Petersburg**'s elegant canals and palaces have drowned under both floodwaters and creeping death. The Necromyce infection bloomed wildly here, accelerated by the wet climate. Survivors cling to fortified islands, trading through risky boat crossings. Every tide could bring salvation—or a drifting horror pulled along by unseen currents. Legends tell of an ancient vault hidden beneath the Hermitage Museum, rumored to hold relics of the old world untouched by plague.

### Western Europe and Surrounds

When the Old World collapsed, **Western Europe**—a cradle of revolution, art, and empire—sank into a slower, quieter ruin than the Americas. Here, pride clashed with desperation. Walled cities tried to shut out the infection, only to find it already seeded within. From the vineyards of France to the factories of Germany, from the markets of Spain to the highlands of Scotland, civilization did not fall all at once—but rot spread at its roots until the proud towers of the West stood empty, gnawed hollow by time and the spores alike.

In tunnels old empires rot beside new spores. Both stink of pride.

**Natural Disasters**

Flooding, heatwaves and landslides.

**Minerals and Resources**

Iron, coal and agriculture.

**Survival Insight**

Ancient aqueducts and tunnels could now serve as transport lines or Gravebloomer habitats.

**Urban tacticians. Memory of empires.**

Survivors from here carry the legacy of resistance and revolution. Players gain a small bonus when scavenging old-world relics, books, or art-based items. City ruins are more familiar terrain, improving evasion slightly in tight urban spaces.

**Starting Inventory**

📖 Preserved Culture Tome - A thick, waterproof-bound anthology of art, music, and forgotten literature. Boosts morale slightly during rests. Can be traded for 2 high-grade rations or a Gravemark token (rumored to open sealed vaults).

#### Berlin, Germany

Once a phoenix city reborn from two world wars and a divided wall, **Berlin** was a beacon of culture and innovation. Its nightlife, its rebellion, its fierce creativity—all went silent beneath the ash-grey skies of the Bloom. Today, remnants of its techno culture echo faintly through crumbling bunkers and empty clubs, as scavenger bands stalk the graffiti-smeared ruins.

#### Paris, France

The City of Light flickered out early in the Collapse. Once a monument to beauty and resilience, **Paris** became a labyrinth of rotting luxury. Ivy-strangled balconies overlook streets flooded with mist. The Eiffel Tower still stands—but corroded and cloaked in fungal blooms, it watches over a city where hope itself decayed faster than the stone facades.

#### Madrid, Spain

**Madrid** burned longer than most. Its fierce independence fueled pockets of resistance that fought for every barrio and alley. But the Blight was patient. Today, Madrid is a ghost city painted with murals of fallen heroes. Its plazas are battlegrounds for desperate survivors, its metro tunnels dark rivers feeding only hunger and fear.

#### Edinburgh, Scotland

Perched on its ancient volcanic rock, **Edinburgh** once blended history and modernity seamlessly. When the infection rose, the city's fortress walls and rugged landscape gave it a longer breath of survival. Now, the Royal Mile is broken and deserted, and mist rolls endlessly through empty streets where old castles look down on a kingdom of ash and memory.

#### London, United Kingdom

Once the heartbeat of a global empire and a symbol of endurance through centuries of conflict, **London** fell not to bombs or war, but to silence. The Necromyce Bloom spread rapidly through its veins—the Underground, once a marvel of engineering, became a maze of suffocation and death. Towering skyscrapers stand hollow now, ghostly silhouettes against a perpetually grey sky. The fog here is heavier, thicker, almost alive—carrying the scents of rot, mildew, and something worse. Survivors whisper of hidden enclaves deep within the remnants of the Tube, fiercely protected and almost impossible to find. But most who wander too deep into the old city's skeleton never return.

#### Reykjavik, Iceland

Iceland's isolation offered brief salvation when the world fell. **Reykjavík** survived longer than most—but eventually, supply shortages and desperate refugees overwhelmed the island's delicate balance. Now, rumors claim that somewhere deep in Iceland's icy heart, a new kind of colony stirs—one untouched by Gravebloomers, but not untouched by desperation.

### Australasia and Surrounds

The southern lands—the sun-drenched coasts of **Australia and the green mountains of New Zealand**—believed distance and isolation would save them. For a time, it did. Refugees flooded in from the dying north. But Necromyce was patient. It traveled unseen in cargo holds and infected flesh. When it finally bloomed in the southern hemisphere, it consumed paradise with the same ruthless hunger, leaving cities silent under endless blue skies.

The soil here never cooled. Not since the ancestors walked barefoot on the lava fields. Some say the heat never left their bones.

**Natural Disasters**

Wildfires, cyclones, droughts and earthquakes.

**Minerals and Resources**

Bauxite, gold and geothermal.

**Survival Insight**

Some tribes may have returned to pre-colonial survival methods, some's geothermal zones allow heated shelters.

**Bushcraft and wind-worn patience.**

Players who begin in Australasia gain a knack for environmental adaptation—making the most of sparse shelter and water. Slightly improved trap-building and fire-making skills, and a reduced penalty when navigating storms or tropical decay zones.

**Starting Inventory**

🪢 Field Repair Kit - Compact bundle with wire, tape, fasteners, and bushcraft tools. Boosts crafting success slightly. Trade value: 12 parts or 1 random weapon upgrade component.

#### Sydney, Australia

The shining jewel of Australia's coast, **Sydney** fell in waves of panic and betrayal. The once-bustling harbor is now choked with rotting ships, their skeletal remains jutting from poisoned waters. Fungal blooms crawl up the steel bones of the Opera House and the Harbour Bridge, and the old suburbs are deadly labyrinths ruled by savage scavenger clans and Blightspore packs that hunt at dusk.

#### Melbourne, Australia

**Melbourne**'s sprawling art districts and iconic laneways became grim mausoleums almost overnight. An early failed quarantine effort turned whole boroughs into festering nests of infection. The survivors that remain here live in isolated towers, their windows shuttered against the toxic winds that sweep down from the Yarra River. Amid the decay, underground markets thrive—trading knowledge, old tech, and dangerous hope.

#### Auckland, New Zealand

**Auckland**, the "City of Sails," once hoped to ride out the apocalypse behind its island borders. It was a fleeting dream. Now its shores are silent, its marinas rotting and its glass towers shattered by nature's slow but steady hand. In the thick, misty hills surrounding the city, communities fight not just against the infected, but against each other, old rivalries flaring in the face of endless desperation.

#### Port Moresby, Papua New Guinea

**Port Moresby** became a paradox of survival. Rugged mountains shielded its people longer than most, but when the infection breached their defenses, it spread like wildfire through the dense settlements. Now, the city's overgrown streets and jungled outskirts are home to fierce tribal enclaves—some fighting to preserve old ways, others embracing the chaos with terrifying new traditions born of plague and despair.

#### Suva, Fiji

Once a vibrant hub of Pacific trade and tropical tourism, **Suva** stood proudly as the heart of Fiji's culture and commerce. Its colorful markets, colonial architecture, and sprawling harbor bustled with life. When the Necromyce Bloom reached these shores, isolation bought Suva a few precious months of uneasy peace. But the infection spread relentlessly across the islands, leaving the once-celebrated capital eerily silent. Now, Suva's jungle-choked streets hide both the remnants of island survivors and strange pockets of Gravebloomer settlements that worship the infection as divine retribution. The ocean, once a lifeline, has become another boundary few dare to cross.

### Asia and Surrounds

**Asia**, the most populous continent, fractured spectacularly under the weight of the Necromyce catastrophe. In the East, ancient cities drowned under fungal blooms and desperate migrations. In the South, chaotic megacities collapsed into feral ruins, while in the North, remnants of order cling to survival in fortified enclaves. Across this vast and broken land, the old boundaries of nation and creed have blurred, replaced by brutal pragmatism—and an ever-present fear of what lurks beyond the crumbling skyline.

Beneath the mountains' silent watch, monks planted ironwood trees and whispered prayers to the old gods. Something answered.

**Natural Disasters**

Earthquakes, typhoons, floods and tsunamis.

**Minerals and Resources**

Rare earth metals, coal and forests.

**Survival Insight**

Some villages and rural strongholds resisted longer due to isolation and centuries of spiritual preservation techniques.

**Precision from chaos. Shadows in numbers.**

Those from this vast region benefit from generational exposure to crowded survival. Minor bonuses in stealth within dense urban ruins, and a slight edge in negotiating or navigating old transport systems like subways and skyways.

**Starting Inventory**

🎭 Gravebloomer Disguise Mask - Replica used in urban cult resistance. Wearing it reduces detection by Mimics slightly. Highly valuable in trade with cult-friendly NPCs.

#### Tokyo, Japan

Once a glowing monument to technology and precision, **Tokyo** fell swiftly. In a city built vertically, the infection raced through the subways and skyscrapers alike, leaving behind towering vertical graveyards. Some of the tallest structures now sway under the weight of fungal overgrowth, while small survivor enclaves cling to the lower ruins, illuminated only by the faint remnants of neon.

#### Shanghai, China

**Shanghai**'s glittering skyline faded beneath the choking breath of Necromyce faster than anyone believed possible. The Huangpu River now carries spores as easily as it once carried commerce. Though the city's upper districts are largely abandoned, rumors whisper of strange new civilizations forming deep within its cavernous underground metro networks.

#### Mumbai, India

**Mumbai** was already a city of extreme contrasts—wealth and poverty colliding block by block. When the infection struck, it exploited every crack in the city's defenses. Crowded slums became death traps; opulent towers became tombs. Yet amidst the chaos, Mumbai's fierce spirit persists. Nomadic bands, many formed from former film stars and artists, now roam the ruins, preserving stories and art as sacred relics of a lost world.

#### Seoul, South Korea

**Seoul** resisted longer than most, fortified by technology, surveillance, and brutal lockdowns. But no wall, no drone, no quarantine could halt the inevitable. Now, Seoul's sprawling urban maze teems with Blightspore nests, while the few surviving humans have taken to living underground, emerging only under cover of night, fearful of both the infected—and each other.

#### Jakarta, Indonesia

**Jakarta**'s tropical humidity became a death sentence when the Necromyce spores arrived. The city drowned under layers of moss, vines, and fungal rot, turning its towering skyline into ghostly green monoliths. Tribes of Gravebloomers now rule the upper levels of long-abandoned skyscrapers, battling each other and whatever horrors still crawl in the steamy depths below.

### Southern Africa and Surrounds

**Southern Africa**, once a land of breathtaking contrasts—from sun-scorched deserts to lush savannahs and coastal paradises—became a battlefield where nature itself turned against its people. In some remote areas, communities resisted the Necromyce Bloom longer than most, shielded by distance and resilience. But no refuge lasted forever. Today, remnants of humanity cling stubbornly to the bones of once-great cities, forging fragile new societies beneath an endless, infected sky.

Gold still glitters beneath Johannesburg—but these days, it's teeth, not coins, that guard it.

**Natural Disasters**

Droughts, flash floods and locust swarms.

**Minerals and Resources**

Gold, diamonds, uranium and fertile plateaus.

**Survival Insight**

Desert clans may control trade in rare isotopes; former mining shafts now house colonies or entomb horrors.

**Sun-hardened. Deep-ground thinkers.**

Born from lands where shelter meant digging, not building—players here have a better grasp of subterranean navigation and structural stability. Slight bonus when creating underground camps or detecting environmental hazards before collapse.

**Starting Inventory**

🛠️ Mining Scanner Fragment - Old tech from a survey drone. Can locate metal-rich ruins or hidden stashes within short range. Can be traded for rare ammo (4 bullets) or a scavenger's relic blade.

#### Cape Town, South Africa

Nestled between mountains and sea, **Cape Town** fought valiantly to shield its people behind hastily constructed quarantine walls. But the infection seeped in through the ports, the farmlands, and the refugee camps. Now, the city is a fractured jewel: crumbling colonial districts swallowed by spores, while isolated colonies cling to the slopes of Table Mountain, wary both of the Blightspores below and each other.

#### Johannesburg, South Africa

Once a powerhouse of commerce and culture, **Johannesburg**'s gleaming skyscrapers became tombstones almost overnight. Mass evacuations turned highways into graveyards of rusted cars and desperate refugees. Today, "Jozi"' is a dangerous sprawl of broken buildings and contested territories, where Gravebloomer clans and uninfected warily navigate a world where gold no longer holds any value—but food, water, and loyalty do.

#### Windhoek, Namibia

The arid expanses around **Windhoek** delayed the spread of infection, giving rise to stubborn pockets of survivors who adapted quickly to the new world. Makeshift fortresses dot the desert sands, while scavengers risk the deadly heat to trade salvaged technology. Windhoek remains a rare prize: a functioning hub of survival—if you can survive the treacherous journey to reach it.

#### Antananarivo, Madagascar

Isolation bought Madagascar time, but not salvation. The capital, **Antananarivo**, was consumed slowly and painfully. Jungle vines now entwine the colonial architecture, while fungal blooms choke the old marketplaces. Strange new sects have risen among the survivors here, blending old island spirituality with grim devotion to surviving the Necromyce, no matter the cost.

#### Kinshasa, Democratic Republic of the Congo

Sprawling along the banks of the Congo River, **Kinshasa** was once a vibrant, chaotic heart of Central Africa. But its vitality became its downfall when Necromyce found its way through its arteries. Bridges collapsed, districts burned, and the river itself became a perilous barrier between the desperate living and the encroaching infected. Today, the ruins of Kinshasa are whispered about with awe and dread: a place where ancient spirits, blooming graveyards, and stubborn colonies cling to the battered edges of survival.

### Northern Africa and Surrounds

Home to deserts older than time and cities built upon the bones of ancient empires. **Northern Africa**, with its vast open spaces, initially resisted the spread better than most. Nomadic tribes, desert fortresses, and hidden oases delayed the inevitable. But trade routes and urban centers crumbled one after another, and the Sahara itself began to swallow the last remnants of humanity like a patient, hungry god.

The Sahara has no borders. But under its skin, oases bloom where blood was spilled.

**Natural Disasters**

Sandstorms, droughts and seismic activity.

**Minerals and Resources**

Phosphates, oil, salt and spice routes.

#### Cairo Egypt

**Cairo**, once a labyrinth of life along the Nile, became a city of tombs once more. The sprawling metropolis was overwhelmed by the infected early on, its ancient streets now echoing with the whispers of the dead. Gravebloomer cults have taken root among the relics of the old dynasties, and scavengers willing to brave the ruins speak of strange, fungal-covered effigies honoring forgotten pharaohs.

#### Algiers, Algeria

**Algiers**' whitewashed buildings once gleamed over the Mediterranean Sea. Now, they are weathered and broken, worn by time and plague. The port city initially attempted isolation, blockading its harbors and fortifying its hills, but the Necromyce found a way through desperate hands. Today, Algiers is a bitter city of fragmented factions, its steep alleys home to smugglers, mercenaries, and a few stubborn dreamers still clinging to civilization.

#### Tripoli, Libya

Already scarred by years of unrest, **Tripoli** was ill-prepared when the infection surged ashore. What order remained collapsed swiftly into chaos. In the ruins of crumbling mosques and gutted apartment blocks, Gravebloomers and survivors alike struggle for control, and ghost stories tell of hidden sanctuaries deep within the old Roman ruins—if one is desperate enough to search for them.

#### Marrakesh, Morocco

The Red City of **Marrakesh**, famed for its vibrant markets and labyrinthine alleys, became a fortress of defiance. For a time, the old medina walls held back the infected, offering one of the last bastions of hope in North Africa. But hope is a brittle thing. Now, Marrakesh stands as a patchwork of fortified compounds and quarantined ruins, where spices, stories, and suspicion all trade at the same price.

#### Lagos, Nigeria

Once Africa's most populous city, **Lagos** was a humming titan of industry, trade, and culture before the Fall. When the infection spread, the city's dense population turned from its greatest strength to its gravest weakness. Entire districts fell overnight, and the mainland and island sectors became battlegrounds. Now, pockets of survivors barter from crumbling skyscrapers and decaying shipping yards, while Gravebloomers are rumored to have established a formidable "Bloom Court" deep within the city's overgrown outskirts.
